> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This is an "event", not a "proposition".** It records **what officials published**, in time order;
> it makes no truth judgement and carries no argument. Much speculation surrounds this timeline
> ("EVALI was really COVID-19", "COVID-19 was in the U.S. much earlier"); this entry downgrades all of it
> to **open questions** and states, for every source, **how deeply this repo verified it**. **When citing,
> give the `status` and the source for each row.**

## Statement

**Event**: in 2019 the United States saw an outbreak of severe lung injury of initially unknown cause,
later named **e-cigarette, or vaping, product use–associated lung injury (EVALI)**. The final official
tally (as of 2020-02-18): **2,807 hospitalisations and 68 deaths** reported nationwide. The mainstream
conclusion was **chemical exposure** (vitamin E acetate and other substances in illicit THC cartridges),
not an infection.

Immediately adjacent in time, at the end of December 2019 Wuhan reported a cluster of "pneumonia of
unknown cause" to WHO and the COVID-19 pandemic followed; then, from 2020, **blood-donation serology
studies** suggested SARS-CoV-2 **may (possibly, and later likely)** have been present in the United States
at **low level** before 2020-01-19 — the date the first U.S. case was reported.

Online discussion often stitches these three strands into claims such as "COVID-19 really started with the
American vaping illness". **This entry makes no such judgement**: it keeps the official timeline, the
official positions and the open questions side by side, so that every claim can be traced to a source.

> [!NOTE]
> **Why record this**: EVALI's confirmed case definition required "negative respiratory viral panel" and
> similar exclusions — and the 2019 panels **could not have included** SARS-CoV-2, which had not yet been
> discovered. That leaves a real **testing gap**. A gap is a blank, not evidence; whether any 2019 EVALI
> case was in fact COVID-19 **cannot be ruled out retrospectively at the testing level**. Whether any
> official systematic retrospective testing was done, and with what result, is listed under "Open
> questions" below.

### 1. EVALI timeline (2017–2020)

| 2018 (control) | **None** of the illicit THC cartridges seized by Minnesota law enforcement that year contained vitamin E acetate (VEA) | ev-094 |

| 2020-03-06 | MMWR publishes the Wisconsin cluster analysis, reconstructing the sharing and transmission details of June–July 2019 | ev-094 |

| Date | What happened | Source |

| 2019-12-31 | WHO's China Country Office is informed of a cluster of "pneumonia of unknown cause" in Wuhan | ev-095 |

| 2020-01-04 | WHO reports the Wuhan cluster of pneumonia cases on social media | ev-095 |
| 2020-01-05 | WHO publishes its first Disease Outbreak News item (DON229) | ev-095 |
| 2020-01-10 | WHO issues interim guidance for countries on detection and case management | ev-095 |
| 2020-01-12 | China publicly shares the SARS-CoV-2 genetic sequence | ev-095 |
| 2020-01-19 | The first U.S. COVID-19 case is reported — the baseline date ("before 19 January 2020") used by both serology studies | ev-096 · ev-098 |

### 3. Retrospective serology and origins (2020–2025)

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2020-11-30 | CDC and Red Cross study published: of 7,389 donations from nine states (2019-12-13–2020-01-17), **106** were pan-Ig reactive and **84 of 90** retested samples had neutralising activity; "SARS-CoV-2 **may** have been introduced into the United States prior to January 19, 2020" | ev-096 |
| 2024-06-28 | OFID follow-up study: of 46,120 donations, **9 confirmed-positive samples** among the 13,364 collected 2019-12-13–2020-01-22; "SARS-CoV-2 was **likely** present in the United States before 19 January 2020" | ev-098 |
| 2025-06-27 | WHO SAGO origins assessment: **all hypotheses remain on the table**; "the weight of available evidence" supports zoonotic spillover (directly from bats or via an intermediate host); key data gaps persist (WHO's requests to China for early sequences, animal-market information and laboratory biosafety information remain unmet) | ev-099 |

### 4. Unresolved contradictions and open questions

1. **The testing gap**: the confirmed EVALI case definition required ruling out respiratory infection, but the 2019 panels **could not have included** SARS-CoV-2 testing, which did not exist. Whether any 2019 EVALI case was actually COVID-19 therefore **cannot be ruled out at the testing level**. **As of this repo's update date, this repo has not found any official publication of systematic retrospective SARS-CoV-2 testing of 2019 EVALI cases** (this reflects what our searches found; it is not a proof of absence).
2. **The limits of "possibly earlier"**: both blood-donation studies use the wording *possibly / likely*, and both note that **at low prevalence, false positives and cross-reactivity cannot be fully excluded**. They support **earlier, low-level** presence — **not** widespread community transmission in 2019, and **not** any link to EVALI.
3. **Origins unresolved**: SAGO (2025) points to zoonotic spillover as "the weight of available evidence" while **key data remain unavailable** and the work is "unfinished"; WHO keeps appealing for information sharing.
4. **The infection chains of the early positive donors are unknown**: where the 2019-12-13–16 antibody-reactive donors were infected, and whether any related cases exist, is answered neither by the two papers nor by the WHO materials.

> [!NOTE]
> **This entry's position on the two common claims:**
> "EVALI was really COVID-19 misdiagnosed" — **no official document says so**; the testing gap is a
> **blank, not evidence**. "The blood studies prove COVID-19 was already widespread in the U.S. in 2019" —
> the studies say possibly / likely and flag false-positive risk; they support **earlier, low-level**
> presence, not "widespread". An event records only "who published what, on which day"; any conclusive
> claim belongs in a **proposition**, with its own evidence.
