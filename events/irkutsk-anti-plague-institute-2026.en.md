## Statement

**Event**: a 28-year-old female laboratory worker (named in media as **Дарья Шипилова / Darya Shipilova**)
at the "Irkutsk Anti-Plague Research Institute of Siberia and the Far East" (Irkutsk APRI) in Shelekhov,
Irkutsk region, Russia, was admitted with severe pneumonia in late September 2026 and **died in the early
hours of 2 October**. Whether she died of **pneumonic plague** was raised by several parties but **has not
been confirmed**; WHO and Russia call it "**pneumonia of undetermined etiology**".

The institute's laboratory and the inpatient unit of Shelekhov District Hospital were quarantined; about
**197 contacts** were placed under medical observation (about 189 of them hospitalised), and several
countries tightened border health checks.

> [!NOTE]
> **What makes this case valuable is that it is both an "unknown-pneumonia event" and an "information event".**
> The authorities withheld the pathogen result for a long time; media and Telegram channels asserted
> "pneumonic plague" first and several countries escalated screening — **and as of the latest update,
> no laboratory result has been published.** This entry keeps the **official account** and the **media reports**
> side by side and does not decide for either.

### 2. Positions (official line vs media reports)

| Issue | Official line | Media reports (not registered as this repo's evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cause of death** | "pneumonia of undetermined etiology"; not plague; no pathogen result published; reportedly classified as "**community-acquired pneumonia**" (UA.NEWS 10-08) | pneumonic plague; a channel claimed a repeat test "confirmed" it |
| **Lab accident** | two independent investigations found **no** biosafety/biosecurity incident | she broke a plague test tube on 25 Sep |
| **Second case** | **fake information** | several outlets reported one; WHO also asked Russia to verify |
| **Quarantine scope** | only the treating hospital; no other facility | four Irkutsk hospitals quarantined for three weeks (denied by the hospitals and by an on-site check); **60+ staff confined inside the institute** (RFE/RL 10-05); **a maternity hospital screened pregnant patients and restricted visitors** (Moscow Times 10-06) |
| **Testing scale** | about 197 contacts (189 hospitalised); observation 60% → "100% completed" | **about 5,000 samples with no dangerous pathogen found** (Moscow Times 10-08, citing Rospotrebnadzor and the governor); one outlet reported "over 100 hospitalised and monitored" (Independent 10-07) — figures differ |
| **Enforcement** | not mentioned | **FSB officers escorted ambulances transporting contacts for examination** (Lyudi Baikala 10-02, via Kyiv Independent) |
| **Official tone** | the situation is "stable" / "calm" | Governor Igor Kobzev: "It was a test, but we passed it in worthy fashion" (Moscow Times 10-08) |
| **Source of infection** | no official account; the worker did not leave the oblast | three theories: lab accident / a Buryatia trip (denied) / a Thailand trip (no evidence) |

### 3. International response

- **WHO**: risk assessment on 10-06; DON on 10-10 saying a lab-associated exposure cannot be fully excluded, recommending further testing (including reference laboratories), and **advising against** any travel or trade restrictions (ev-064 · ev-067).
- **ECDC**: on 10-06, no secondary cases and no evidence of sustained transmission; its 10-09 weekly report said the **information is insufficient to draw a firm conclusion on the risk to the EU population** (ev-068).
- **United States**: the State Department said it was monitoring closely; on 10-07 it delivered a formal diplomatic complaint (démarche) asking Russia for more information (media reports).
- **Neighbouring countries**: Mongolia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Indonesia tightened border/port health checks; the CDC of Hulunbuir in China's Inner Mongolia issued a plague risk advisory (all per media reports).

### 4. Unresolved contradictions and open questions

1. **Pathogen unknown**: Russia says "not plague" and reportedly classifies the death as "**community-acquired pneumonia**" — but that is a diagnosis category, not a pathogen; **no pathogen result has ever been published**, and it has not said what killed her. WHO has asked; **nothing has been released**.
2. **"Not plague" and "pneumonic plague" coexist**: the official line and parts of the media point in opposite directions; **this repo cannot decide who is right**.
3. **"Was vaccinated" lacks detail**: Russia says she "was vaccinated against the high-threat pathogens she worked with" but **does not say which**.
4. **Why hospitalise 189 contacts** for a diagnosis officials call non-contagious — a point questioned from outside.
5. **Inconsistent sequencing**: Russia told WHO on 10-06 that observation of all contacts had ended, while Rospotrebnadzor said publicly on 10-07 "more than 90%".
6. **Was the observation period long enough**: WHO says that without a confirmed diagnosis it **cannot independently judge whether 7 days was enough to exclude all plausible causes**.

> [!NOTE]
> **This entry records the official line, the media reports and their disagreements only; it does not decide
> whether it was plague.** That would be a proposition's job (and would need stronger evidence); an event only
> answers "who said what, on which day".
