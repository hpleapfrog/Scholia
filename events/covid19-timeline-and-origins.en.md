> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This is an "event", not a "proposition".** It records **what officials published**, in time order;
> it makes no truth judgement and carries no argument. Much speculation surrounds this timeline
> ("EVALI was really COVID-19", "COVID-19 was in the U.S. much earlier", "the virus spread through U.S.
> military blood programmes"); this entry downgrades all of it to **open questions** and states, for every
> source, **how deeply this repo verified it**. **When citing, give the `status` and the source for each row.**

## Statement

**Event**: in 2019 the United States saw an outbreak of severe lung injury of initially unknown cause,
later named **e-cigarette, or vaping, product use–associated lung injury (EVALI)**. The final official
tally (as of 2020-02-18): **2,807 hospitalisations and 68 deaths** reported nationwide. The mainstream
conclusion was **chemical exposure** (vitamin E acetate and other substances in illicit THC cartridges),
not an infection.

Immediately adjacent in time, at the end of December 2019 WHO was informed of a cluster of "pneumonia of
unknown cause" in Wuhan, and the COVID-19 pandemic followed — WHO declared a PHEIC on 2020-01-30, countries
moved into states of emergency and lockdowns in March 2020, and on 2023-05-05 WHO declared that COVID-19
**no longer constitutes a PHEIC**. Then, from 2020, **blood-donation serology, wastewater monitoring
and retrospective testing of stored samples** suggested SARS-CoV-2 **may — possibly, and in some studies likely —**
have been present at **low level** in several countries before 2020-01-19 — the day the first U.S. case
presented for care.
**How the virus first entered the human population remains undetermined.**

Online discussion often stitches these official strands into claims such as "COVID-19 really started with
the American vaping illness". **This entry makes no such judgement**: it keeps the official timeline, the
official positions and the open questions side by side, so that every claim can be traced to a source.

> [!NOTE]
> **Why record this**: EVALI's confirmed case definition required "negative respiratory viral panel" and
> similar exclusions — and the 2019 panels **could not have included** SARS-CoV-2, which had not yet been
> discovered. That leaves a real **testing gap**. A gap is a blank, not evidence; whether any 2019 EVALI
> case was in fact COVID-19 **cannot be ruled out retrospectively at the testing level**. Whether any
> official systematic retrospective testing was done, and with what result, is listed under "Open
> questions" below.

### 3. Retrospective testing and origins assessment (2020–2025)

| Date | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2020-05-03 | France: IJAA paper online — retrospective retesting of stored respiratory samples from 58 ILI patients admitted to an ICU in northern Paris **2019-12-02–2020-01-16**: 14 stored samples analysed, one positive by RT-PCR — a 42-year-old man seen in the emergency ward on **2019-12-27** (last foreign travel 2019-08) | ev-111 |
| 2020-08-15 | Italy: wastewater study online — SARS-CoV-2 detectable in wastewater from Milan and Turin as early as **2019-12-18** (Bologna 2020-01-29); all 24 control samples from 2018-09–2019-06 negative | ev-112 |
| 2020-11-11 | Italy: Tumori paper online — **111 of 959 (11.6%)** asymptomatic screening participants' blood samples positive for RBD antibodies, earliest from **2019-09** (14% that month); Lombardy highest at 53.2% | ev-110 |
| 2020-11-30 | CDC and Red Cross study published: of 7,389 donations from nine states (2019-12-13–2020-01-17), **106** were pan-Ig reactive and **84 of 90** retested samples had neutralising activity; "SARS-CoV-2 **may** have been introduced into the United States prior to January 19, 2020" | ev-096 |
| 2020-12-08 | Italy: EID paper online — an oropharyngeal swab taken on **2019-12-05** from a 4-year-old boy in Milan (sampled for suspected measles) retrospectively tests positive for SARS-CoV-2 RNA (Sanger sequencing, GenBank MW303957; the only positive among 39 specimens from the period); the authors note the finding **cannot pin down the strain's origin** | ev-117 |
| 2021-01-14–02-10 | WHO-convened joint study: the international team carries out a 28-day study visit in Wuhan (17 Chinese and 17 international experts) | ev-101 · ev-103 |
| 2021-02-09 | Joint expert press conference (UN News): a laboratory leak is "extremely unlikely"; transmission via an **intermediate host** "remains the most likely pathway"; cold-chain introduction "needs further exploration" | ev-103 |
| 2021-03-08 | Brazil: STOTEN paper online — a sewage sample from Florianopolis collected on **2019-11-27** tests positive for SARS-CoV-2 RNA (≈5.49 log10 copies/L), confirmed by an independent laboratory and sequencing; the authors conclude SARS-CoV-2 was "likely circulating undetected in the community in Brazil **since November 2019**" | ev-118 |
| 2021-03-30 | Joint study report released: WHO — "**all hypotheses remain on the table**"; about **28%** of early cases linked to the Huanan market, **23%** to other Wuhan markets and **45%** with no market exposure; "the role of the Huanan market in the origin of the pandemic, and how the virus was introduced into the market, **cannot be determined conclusively**"; the Director-General said the team's assessment of the laboratory-accident hypothesis had not been broad enough and that more data and research were needed; he added that additional task forces with specialist expertise might be required and that he was willing to send them (UN News) | ev-101 · ev-103 |
| 2021-06-15 | U.S.: NIH "All of Us" news release — **9 of 24,079** participants' blood samples (2020-01-02–03-18) positive on two sequential serology platforms (earliest 01-07 Illinois, 01-08 Massachusetts; five states; 7 before their state's first confirmed case) | ev-113 |
| 2021-07-16 | At the **Member State Information Session on Origins**, the WHO Director-General announces a permanent **Scientific Advisory Group for the Origins of Novel Pathogens (SAGO)**; the proposed next-phase studies circulated to Member States include **audits of relevant laboratories**, studies of the earliest areas of circulation, Wuhan animal markets and animal trace-back (including early viral sequences), and he calls on China to share all relevant data | ev-134 |
| 2021-10-13 | WHO announces the proposed members of the **Scientific Advisory Group for the Origins of Novel Pathogens (SAGO)** — opening a public comment period; the group will advise on a global framework for studies into the origins of emerging and re-emerging pathogens, including SARS-CoV-2 | ev-132 |
| 2022-06-09 | SAGO publishes its **first preliminary report** — the report describes itself as "ongoing work", with further reports to follow as discussions continue | ev-133 |
| 2022-07-26 | Two Science papers published together (Worobey et al.; Pekar et al.) — the earliest known cases, including those without reported direct links, are geographically centred on the Huanan market; lineages A and B suggest **at least two** separate cross-species spillovers, the first around **2019-11-18**; wide circulation before November 2019 is unlikely (each paper later received an erratum; see Basis) | ev-119 |
| 2024-06-28 | OFID follow-up study: of 46,120 donations, **9 confirmed-positive samples** among the 13,364 collected 2019-12-13–2020-01-22; "SARS-CoV-2 was **likely** present in the United States before 19 January 2020" | ev-098 |
| 2024-09-19 | Cell: reanalysis of environmental samples from the Huanan market (early 2020) — market-linked viral diversity is consistent with market emergence; positivity increases near and within one wildlife stall, and **all positive samples there contain wildlife DNA** (civets, bamboo rats, raccoon dogs and others) | ev-120 |
| 2025-06-27 | WHO SAGO origins assessment: **all hypotheses must remain on the table**; "the weight of available evidence" supports zoonotic spillover (directly from bats or via an intermediate host); key data gaps persist (WHO's requests to China for early sequences, animal-market information and laboratory biosafety information remain unmet) | ev-099 |

### 7. Unresolved contradictions and open questions

1. **The testing gap**: the confirmed EVALI case definition required ruling out respiratory infection, but the 2019 panels **could not have included** SARS-CoV-2 testing, which did not exist. Whether any 2019 EVALI case was actually COVID-19 therefore **cannot be ruled out at the testing level**. **As of this repo's update date, this repo has not found any official publication of systematic retrospective SARS-CoV-2 testing of 2019 EVALI cases** (this reflects what our searches found; it is not a proof of absence).
2. **The limits of "possibly earlier"**: both blood-donation studies use the wording *possibly / likely*, and both note that **at low prevalence, false positives and cross-reactivity cannot be fully excluded**. They support **earlier, low-level** presence — **not** widespread community transmission in 2019, and **not** any link to EVALI. The same applies to the retrospective studies from Italy (blood, wastewater, a child's swab), France, Brazil and "All of Us" — their sampling frames, assay characteristics and false-positive risks differ; this repo does not merge evidence grades across studies. Each paper states its own limitations.
3. **Origins unresolved**: the 2021 joint study said the role of the Huanan market **cannot be determined conclusively**; SAGO (2025) points to zoonotic spillover as "the weight of available evidence" while **key data remain unavailable** and the work is "unfinished"; WHO keeps appealing for information sharing. Two Science papers (2022) and one Cell paper (2024) analysing Huanan-market samples support a market-introduction scenario (see section 3).
4. **The infection chains of the early positives are unknown**: for the 2019-12-13–16 antibody-reactive donors, the Italian antibody-positive subjects from 2019-09, the child whose Milan swab was positive on 2019-12-05, the French patient of 2019-12-27 and the local transmission implied by the Brazilian sewage detection of 2019-11-27, no paper or WHO material says where the infections came from or whether related cases exist.
5. **The boundary of "U.S. military facility / blood programme" claims**: from 2021, media articles proposed specific chains such as "the virus spread from Fort Detrick and other bases to Europe via the U.S. Armed Services Blood Program (ASBP)"; **as far as this repo's searches go, no official investigative conclusion or official statement supports these chains**. Nor has this repo found any published retrospective SARS-CoV-2 testing of 2019 U.S. military personnel serum samples. The only official records are the facts in section 4. (This reflects what our searches found; it is not a proof of absence.)
