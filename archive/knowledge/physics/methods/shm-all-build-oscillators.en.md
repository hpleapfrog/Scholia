## What the method does

Hands before names:

> `[BL] [OL] [AL] Find springs or rubber bands with different amounts of stiffness. Ask students to attach weights to these to construct oscillators.`

Then the terms, via three separate questions:

> `Introduce the terms frequency and time period. Ask students to observe how the stiffness of the spring affects them. How does mass of the system affect them? How does the initial force applied affect them?`

Three questions, **one variable each**: stiffness, mass, initial force. The textbook gives no answers — they
are supposed to come out of the students' own oscillators.

Before that: `[BL] Review the concept of force.` and `[BL] [OL] [AL] Introduce Hooke's law and force constant`.
Without a restoring force, students see "going back and forth", not "being pulled back".

## Contraindications

| Not suitable for | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| a classroom with **no springs or rubber bands** | all three questions need a physical oscillator | **strong** |
| students who can already write `x(t) = A cos(ωt+φ)` | they need phase and energy instead | **medium** |
| students who need the period formula now | this produces no formula | **medium** |

## Risks

| Where it goes wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| changing two things at once (stiffer spring **and** heavier mass) | the three questions are separate; mixed changes cannot be attributed |
| handing out `T = 2π√(m/k)` first | observe-then-name is the order; the formula first kills all three questions |
| only doing springs | "all oscillation involves force" needs several oscillators |
