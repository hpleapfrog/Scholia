## What the method does

Three sentences, one gap left for the student:

> `[AL] The mass number of the nucleus is conserved, but is the mass? Mention that that mass of a proton is slightly less than the mass of the neutron. Have students consider where that mass goes. Note—Not all of it goes to the electron.`

Counted quantity vs weighed quantity; then the `Note` **closes off the favourite wrong answer**
("the missing mass became the electron").

## Contraindications

| Not suitable for | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students without decay equations | "mass number is conserved" has nothing to stand on | **strong** |
| students about to learn mass defect | this produces a **question**, not `E = mc²` | **medium** |
| a balancing-only lesson | balancing needs mass number only | **medium** |

## Risks

| Where it goes wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| answering "the defect becomes energy" immediately | the source stops at "where does it go"; answering removes the question |
| agreeing with "it became the electron" | `Note—Not all of it goes to the electron` exists to block it |
| treating mass number as mass | the entire practice is "counts conserve, weights do not" |
