## Method

Push students to the conclusion “a starting point must be defined first” with **a chain of progressive questions**:

1. ask “what is position” — using a toy car or any object
2. ask “how do you know it has moved”
3. lead them to the idea of “a defined starting point”
4. then bring in the “numbered line” as a way of **quantifying** motion

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Start by asking what position is and how it is defined. You can use a toy car or other object.
> Then ask how they know the object has moved. Lead them to the idea of a defined starting point.
> Then bring in the concept of a numbered line as a way of quantifying motion.`

**How it differs from the BL tier**: BL creates a contradiction **from multiple observation points**; OL advances **through the teacher's question chain**.
Both do the same thing — make the students themselves need “a reference frame” — but they advance by different means.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| tier | **OL** (textbook marker `[OL]`) |
| prerequisite | able to follow the Q&A; able to accept the abstraction of a “numbered line” |
| teacher's role | **leading** — if the question chain breaks, nothing moves |
| cognitive load | medium — the load sits on **abstraction** (the numbered line) |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this one carries across tiers | **weak** — “the textbook did not specify” ≠ “not applicable”, it is merely **untested** |

> [!WARNING]
> **This entry has no population contraindication backed by strong evidence.**
>
> The textbook offers its suggestion only for this tier and **does not say the method is ineffective for other tiers**.
> Treating “the textbook did not mention it” as “not applicable” is **over-inference** — so it is honestly marked **weak**, nothing is invented.

## Risks

> [!NOTE]
> **The textbook attaches no cautions of any kind to this method.**
>
> This is a **gap**, not “confirmed to have no contraindications”:
> the textbook only warns elsewhere (in the same section) that `students may assume that a reference frame is a background`,
> but does not state whether that risk likewise exists at the OL tier.

| Known adjacent risk | Note |
|---|---|
| the reference frame being misunderstood as the “background” rather than the “viewing vantage point” | raised in the same section by the textbook's Misconception tag, **without distinguishing tiers** — **conservatively, assume it exists at the OL tier too** |
| the question chain depending on the teacher's on-the-spot judgement | the textbook gives a sequence of questions, but no branch for “what to do when students answer wrongly” |
