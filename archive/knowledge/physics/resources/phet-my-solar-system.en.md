> `This simulation allows you to create your own solar system so that you can see how changing
> distances and masses determines the orbits of planets. Click Help for instructions.`

> `Give the students ample time to manipulate this animation. It may take some time to get the
> parameters adjusted so that they can ...`

## Argument

### R2 · Why this resource is worth holding (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the simulation's teaching value lies in making **distance and mass** adjustable variables
- **Premise**: the key restriction of Kepler's third law `r³/T² = GM/4π²` is
  **that it holds only for the same central body** (the textbook marks it with `Emphasize`, ev-031)
- **Reasoning**: a resource that lets you **change the mass M by hand** turns "change the central body and the ratio changes" from a sentence into something observable —
  **this is more likely to fix that restriction than lecturing**
- **Evidence**: ev-032, ev-031
- **Attacked**:
  - **"more likely" is my inference, with no classroom data**
  - the textbook itself warns **adjusting parameters takes time** (`It may take some time`) —
    **the adjustment cost may eat into teaching time**, an unquantified risk
- **Attribution**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-05
- **To be added**: actual adjustment time; a pre/post test of the "same central body" restriction
