> **English version of [`离心力是一个真实的力.md`](离心力是一个真实的力.md).**

## Statement

Students usually say:

> "As soon as the car turns, I get thrown against the door — so there is an outward force
> pushing me, and it's called centrifugal force."

**The sensation is real. The conclusion is not.** The textbook names this confusion:

> `Some students might be confused between centripetal force and centrifugal force. Centrifugal force is not a real force but the result of an accelerating reference frame, such as a turning car or the spinning Earth. Centrifugal force refers to a fictional center fleeing force.`

The correct account:

| What you feel | What happens |
|---|---|
| Your body pressed against the door | Your body keeps going **straight** (Newton's first law) while the car has already turned |
| "Something pushes me out" | **Nothing does.** The car (and the door) accelerates inward; your body still has its original straight-line motion |
| — | The force that *does* turn you points **at the centre**, provided by the seat and the door |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Centrifugal force is not an arrow you may draw on a free-body diagram.**
>
> The textbook files it as `a fictitious force` and states its origin plainly:
> `an accelerating reference frame` (a turning car, the spinning Earth). In other words it appears
> only if you insist on doing the bookkeeping **inside** that accelerating frame.
> Seen from the ground, the only arrows are the real inward ones.

## Argument

### A1 · "Not a real force" is the textbook's explicit position (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: in an inertial frame, centrifugal force has no agent, so it is not a real force
- **Premise**: the textbook calls it `fictitious` and attributes it to an `accelerating reference frame` (ev-081)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. A real force needs an **agent**; ask "who is pushing you" and the answer is the door (inward) or the seat
  2. There is no agent in the outward direction — outward motion is what happens when **no** force acts
  3. Hence the textbook records it as a product of the frame, not an interaction
- **Evidence**: ev-081
- **Attacked**: **"fictitious" is easily read as "so it is wrong and must not be mentioned"** —
  that is not what the textbook means, and not what this entry claims. In a non-inertial frame the
  inertial force makes the equations work; the entry only denies it a place on an inertial-frame
  free-body diagram.
- **Attribution**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-18

### A2 · A true sensation, a false inference (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the reported sensation needs re-attribution, not denial
- **Premise**: the textbook describes it as a `fictional center fleeing force` — the "fleeing" direction
  is exactly where the sensation comes from (ev-081)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. During a turn the body really does move outward **relative to the car** — observable
  2. Reading that relative motion as "an outward force" is one step too many: relative displacement
     needs no force to explain it
  3. Only after re-attribution does "why a force must point inward" make sense
- **Evidence**: ev-081
- **Attacked**: no attacks yet
