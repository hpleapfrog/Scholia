## What the method does

The textbook treats this confusion as **expected**, hence `Remind`:

> `[BL] Students may confuse the terms mass number and atomic number. Remind them that the mass is based on both protons and neutrons … The atomic number differentiates between two different atoms, which only protons can do.`

| Quantity | Set by | Distinguishes |
|---|---|---|
| mass number `A` | protons **+** neutrons | isotopes |
| atomic number `Z` | protons only | **the element** |

Then `A few examples showing pictures of nuclei and having students identify the mass and atomic numbers`
— let them count. Finally the追问: `If each atom of carbon has an atomic number of 6, then what is the
value of including the mass number at all?` (answer: isotopes).

## Contraindications

| Not suitable for | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students without element symbols and proton number | "atomic number identifies the element" has no footing | **strong** |
| students who need decay-equation balancing | this fixes naming, not balancing | **medium** |
| students outside BL | `[AL]` asks whether mass itself is conserved | **medium** |

## Risks

| Where it goes wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| teaching only `A = Z + N` | the source gives **criteria**, not algebra; the confusion survives the equation |
| not correcting "atomic number also depends on neutrons" | `which only protons can do` exists to break exactly that |
| skipping "then what use is the mass number" | that question is what pushes students to isotopes |
