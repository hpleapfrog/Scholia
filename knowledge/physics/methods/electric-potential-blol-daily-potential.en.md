## What the method does

The skeleton is **one everyday word → one concept already learned → one restriction**.

### 1. Ask what "potential" means in daily life

> `[BL] Ask students to define how potential is used in everyday life. Draw the analogy between this and potential energy.`

"Someone has potential" = **not yet, but the conditions are there**. That nuance is the whole point.

### 2. Review the definition sentence

> `[OL] [AL] Review the concept of potential energy as being the potential an object has to do work by virtue of its position with respect to another object.`

**"by virtue of its position"** — position, not speed, not "what it contains". This is the hinge for
every later analogy.

### 3. Add the restriction

> `[OL] Remind students that gravitational potential energy is only meaningful when we deal with differences in energy. In other words, a reference position is chosen…`

The textbook says `Remind` — students have learned it and **will forget it**.

## Conditions

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level | **BL / OL** (the `[BL]` and `[OL]` prompts combined) |
| Prerequisite | gravitational potential energy; Coulomb's law |
| Load | medium — one word carries three layers |

## Contraindications

| Not suitable for | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who never learned gravitational potential energy | steps 2–3 rest on it; `Remind` is an empty action for someone who was never told | **strong** |
| students who need to compute `U = kq₁q₂/r` now | produces no calculation skill | **medium** |
| students outside BL/OL | `[AL]` adds "the reference is often at infinity", which this entry does not carry | **medium** |

## Risks

| Where it goes wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| reading "potential" as "amount of energy" | the everyday sense is **not yet realised**, which is exactly "depends on position"; once reworded, the analogy is lost |
| skipping the reference point | a potential energy has no absolute value; `Remind` shows this is old ground, not new |
| introducing `V = U/q` too early | the textbook `Emphasize the difference` only at the end; reverse the order and the two terms merge |
