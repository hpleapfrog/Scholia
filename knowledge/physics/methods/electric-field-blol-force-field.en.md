## What the method does

The module treats "field" as **a word students have already met**, then swaps in the physics meaning.

### 1. Ask about movies first

> `Ask students whether they have seen movies that use the concept of fields as in force fields. Have them describe how such fields work.`

Students will describe "a region where you get pushed or blocked" — that is the intuition to build on.

### 2. Switch to gravity

> `Describe how gravity can be thought of as a field that surrounds a mass and with which other masses interact. Explain that electric fields are very similar to gravitational fields.`

The order is **movie force field → gravitational field → electric field**. The middle step is what
connects a fictional field to one they already accept.

### 3. Field lines start somewhere specific

> `[BL] [OL] Point out that all electric field lines originate from the charge.`

The textbook says `Point out`, which signals **the thing students most often miss** when reading a diagram.

## Conditions

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level | **BL / OL** (one prompt, both tags) |
| Prerequisite | Coulomb's law; gravitation |
| Load | low to medium — the load is "same word, new meaning" |

## Contraindications

| Not suitable for | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who have **not** met gravitation | the middle link of the analogy breaks; "field" collapses back to the movie image | **strong** |
| students fluent with `E = F/q` already | produces no calculation skill | **medium** |
| students outside BL/OL | the `[AL]` prompt asks a different question (counting lines) | **medium** |

## Risks

| Where it goes wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| treating the movie field as the **definition** | the text immediately adds `it differs somewhat from what you see in movies` — the movie is a door, not the answer |
| drawing field lines without "they start at the charge" | the textbook singles it out with `Point out` |
| skipping the gravitational step | without it the electric field becomes "one more concept to memorise" rather than "the same thing with a different source" |
