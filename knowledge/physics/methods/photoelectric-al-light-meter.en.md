## What the method does

> `[AL] Ask students why a light meter would be useful in a camera. How could the number of electrons emitted from the light meter control the camera's iris? Have students draw a diagram of the camera that may demonstrate this effect.`

Three moves: why useful → **how electron number sets the aperture** → draw it.

The emphasis: what controls the iris is the **number** of electrons, not their energy. That single line
separates "intensity → current" from "frequency → maximum kinetic energy".

## Contraindications

| Not suitable for | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students without the two regularities | the task is precisely to tell number from energy | **strong** |
| a lesson aimed only at the quantum argument | this is application, not the quantum step | **medium** |
| an environment where drawing is impractical | step three requires `draw a diagram` | **medium** |

## Risks

| Where it goes wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| saying "the meter measures the energy of light" | the prompt asks about electron **number** |
| merging it with "brighter means faster" | this is the place to break that misconception |
| skipping the diagram | the diagram is the product of the practice |
