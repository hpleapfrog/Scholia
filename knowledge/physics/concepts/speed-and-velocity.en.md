> [!TIP]
> **After reading this entry you should** be able to distinguish speed from velocity (velocity carries direction), and to identify the difference between everyday usage and the scientific definition

## Definition

- **Speed** is the ratio of distance to time, magnitude only.
- **Velocity** is the ratio of displacement to time, **carrying direction**.

In everyday language the two are often conflated (even teachers' speech reinforces the confusion), while the scientific definitions distinguish them strictly.

## Prerequisite note

**Prerequisite: [reference frame](frame-of-reference.en.md)**

"Which stretch of distance / which interval of time" presupposes first deciding **from where you observe**.
Without a reference frame first established, "displacement" has no way to be defined — and the entire difference between velocity and speed lies in displacement.

## Argument

### K2 · Grounds for the prerequisite relation (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the reference frame is a prerequisite of speed and velocity
- **Premise**: the definition of velocity depends on displacement, and displacement depends on the reference frame (the textbook establishes the reference frame in §2.1 before covering velocity in §2.2)
- **Reasoning**: the textbook's section order puts §2.1 reference frame / distance and displacement before §2.2 speed and velocity
