> [!TIP]
> **After reading this entry you should** be able to state Kepler's three laws, and to use r³/T² only to compare bodies around the **same central body**

## Definition

**Kepler's three laws** describe the **pattern** of planetary motion:

1. **the orbit is an ellipse**, with the Sun at one of its foci
2. **the area law**: the line joining the planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times ⇒ the planet moves faster when it is closer to the Sun
3. **the period law**: `r³/T² = GM/4π²`, the ratio is determined only by **the mass M of the central body**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **They are "laws", not a "theory".**
>
> The textbook explicitly requires making this distinction clear (`[AL]`, ev-031):
> **a law describes a pattern that recurs in nature; a theory provides an explanation for the pattern.**
> **Kepler supplied the pattern and no explanation at all** — the explanation came later from Newton.
>
> This distinction is not rhetoric; it decides what this section should teach and what it should not.

- **Reasoning**: it has independent teaching content (three laws), an independent difficulty (law vs. theory,

- **Evidence**: ev-031
