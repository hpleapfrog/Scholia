## Argument

### A1 · M1 crossed from "the authors' expectation" to "occurrence rate" (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: M1's **original wording** is invalid reasoning — it claims more than the premise can support
- **Premise**: M1's grounds is an **imperative** in the textbook:
  `Discuss the law of conservation of energy and dispel any misconceptions … such is the idea that
  moving objects just slow down naturally` (ev-028)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. the imperative says **"what should be done"**, while the claim says **"what actually happens"** (students **easily** form it).
     **From "what should be done" one cannot infer "what actually happens".**
  2. `such is the idea that…` is only an **example** of `any misconceptions` —
     it gives this specific misconception no **weight**. The "**dedicated**" in "needs dedicated intervention" has even less grounds:
     the textbook writes it in the **same** teaching hint as "discuss the conservation law", i.e. **a passing remark**.
  3. **It directly conflicts with the entry's own `## Frequency`** — that section says
     "cannot prove the occurrence rate … **the former must not impersonate the latter**", and M1 does exactly that impersonation.
- **Evidence**: ev-028
- **Attacked**: none (this entry has not yet received an attack)
- **Attribution**: AI subagent proposed · @hpleapfrog reviewed · 2026-10-05

### A2 · M2 is a proposition about the student's internal state, yet not one word of the evidence discusses students (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: M2 has **zero proof** — it is not an argument supported by evidence but **rhetoric**
- **Premise**: M2 asserts "students see the real phenomenon, they only **attribute** friction's effect to the object's property"
- **Reasoning**:
  1. this is an **empirical proposition** about **what students actually observed and to whom they attributed the cause**.
     Yet the entry's evidence is just two pieces of **textbook text** — **not one sentence in the textbook is a measurement of students**.
     The whole entry has no diagnostic instrument, no interview, no option distribution, no pre/post test.
  2. the first half, "with friction the object slows down", is **correct physics**, but it is **the author's own knowledge**,
     not a fact provided by any evidence; and **"the phenomenon is so" does not imply "this is what students observed"**.
  3. **It conflicts with `## Statement`**: the statement says students think slowing "**needs no reason**";
     M2 says students **know** friction and merely treat it as a property. **If a student truly treats friction as the cause, they are not "thinking no reason is needed".**
  4. so "**the danger is not that it is wrong, but that it is 'right'**" inflates
     "the ordinary prediction happens to be true" into "the essence of this misconception is correct" — **different scopes**.
- **Evidence**: ev-028, ev-025 (**both same-source**, both from OpenStax《Physics》)
- **Attacked**: none
- **Note: this entry does not claim the claim is false either** — the physics-education literature does contain a view that the misconception's cause is
  "failing to identify friction as a force". **But that is the literature's words, not this entry's evidence.**
  This attack targets "**zero proof within the entry**", not "the claim is wrong".
- **Attribution**: AI subagent proposed · @hpleapfrog reviewed · 2026-10-05

### A3 · M3's "only" is a uniqueness claim, and the entry has zero comparative data (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: M3's "**only**" has no grounds to support it
- **Premise**: M3 asserts the pedagogy for correcting the misconception is **unique**
- **Reasoning**:
  1. to get "only", one needs negative results on the **excluded alternatives**.
     Yet the entry does not even **define** the excluded practice — what classroom behavior does "'you observed wrong'" concretely refer to?
  2. the only version with grounds is "**the textbook suggests correcting that way**". From "the textbook suggests" to "uniquely effective" the whole stretch of comparative data is missing.
  3. **conflicts with another repo entry**: `M1` of [conservation of mechanical energy · OL: friction heat](../../methods/mech-energy-ol-friction-heat.en.md)
     explicitly writes "**'textbook-specified' ≠ 'measured effective'**; and there is no data on whether the misconception really disappears once named" —
     and "point out the ignored item" **is exactly** that teaching method. **The same method, treated as established in `M3`, admits no data in the 教法 entry.**
- **Evidence**: ev-028
- **Attacked**: none
- **Attribution**: AI subagent proposed · @hpleapfrog reviewed · 2026-10-05
