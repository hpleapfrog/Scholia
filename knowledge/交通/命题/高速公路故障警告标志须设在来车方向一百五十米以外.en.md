## Statement

Article 68 of the Road Traffic Safety Law of the PRC gives one definite number for breakdown handling on a **motorway**:

> 机动车在高速公路上发生故障时，应当依照本法第五十二条的有关规定办理；但是，
> **警告标志应当设置在故障车来车方向一百五十米以外**，
> 车上人员应当迅速转移到右侧路肩上或者应急车道内，并且迅速报警。(Art. 68, para. 1)

The same article covers the case where the vehicle cannot be driven:

> 机动车在高速公路上发生故障或者交通事故，无法正常行驶的，**应当由救援车、清障车拖曳、牵引**。(Art. 68, para. 2)

The **ordinary-road** clause it cross-refers to is written very differently:

> 机动车在道路上发生故障，需要停车排除故障时，……在来车方向**设置警告标志等措施扩大示警距离**，必要时迅速报警。(Art. 52)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The number "one hundred and fifty metres" belongs to motorways only.**
>
> Article 52, for ordinary roads, says "**take measures to extend the warning distance**" —
> a **goal with no number**; Article 68, for motorways, says "**more than one hundred and fifty metres**" —
> a **measurable obligation**.
>
> **Applying 150 m to an ordinary road, or assuming an ordinary road must also be 150 m,
> are both misreadings.**

## Arguments

### A1 · The text itself gives the number, and distinguishes road types (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: one hundred and fifty metres is **the statute's explicit text**, and **motorways only**
- **Premise**: Article 68 cross-refers to Article 52 first, then uses "**however**" to insert 150 m as the exceptional number; Article 52 itself carries no number, only "extend the warning distance" (ev-051)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. This is "**what the obligation is, what the number is**" — exactly what the [traffic](../README.en.md) subject collects
  2. It is **falsifiable by the reader**: open the official page and search "第六十八条" against "第五十二条"
  3. the word "**however**" is itself the boundary — the cross-reference is the default, 150 m is the **motorway's special provision**
- **Evidence**: ev-051
- **Attacked by**: see A2
- **Signed**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-12

### A2 · This entry claims one version only, and has not checked the implementing regulations (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: this entry claims "**the version amended on 2021-04-29 says so**", **not "it still holds now"**
- **Premise**: the official page carries **no revision number**, only a publication date and the three-amendment history (ev-051)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. the law **has already been amended three times** — this class of text **does get replaced**
  2. and this repo has **no mechanism** to detect a further amendment
  3. writing "still holds now" into the statement would be **beyond the evidence**
- **Evidence**: ev-051
- **Attacked by**: **this entry has checked neither the Road Traffic Safety Law Implementing Regulations nor any national standard.**
  "**How far on an ordinary road, actually**" — the statute only says "extend the warning distance";
  **the specific distance lives in the implementing regulations or a standard, which this repo has not registered,
  so this entry cannot answer it.**
  Likewise, operational details — **whether the vehicle should first be moved to the shoulder,
  how occupants should transfer** — this entry only relays what the statute says,
  **with no operational advice of its own**.
- **Signed**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-12
- **To fill in**: **the text of the Road Traffic Safety Law Implementing Regulations** (not yet registered here).
  With it, ordinary roads get a fillable distance, and this entry's boundary can be stated precisely.

## Why this counts as real information rather than noise

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A motorway sign tells you the speed limit, but not how far the triangle goes after a breakdown.**
>
> What you can see on site is **a motorway and a stopped vehicle**;
> what the law writes down is **a measurable distance** (more than one hundred and fifty metres)
> and **a checkable division of work** (rescue and recovery vehicles do the towing).
>
> **This repo's mission is to strip noise and hold true information.**
> That contrast is the test: **what is visible on site, this repo need not repeat;
> what is invisible on site yet written in law is what belongs here.**
> "Is 150 m mandatory or a suggestion" — the question has no answer on any sign,
> while the statute says "shall".
