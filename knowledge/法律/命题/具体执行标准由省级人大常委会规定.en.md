## Statement

The Road Traffic Safety Law of the PRC splits "how much is the fine" into **two layers**:

| Layer | Who sets it | Where it is written |
|---|---|---|
| **The range** | the NPC Standing Committee (the statute itself) | Articles 89, 90, and others |
| **The specific enforcement standard within that range** | **the standing committee of the province-level people's congress** | **Article 123** |

Original text (short quote):

> 省、自治区、直辖市人民代表大会常务委员会可以根据本地区的实际情况，在本法规定的罚款幅度内，规定具体的执行标准。(Art. 123)

And what gets localised is exactly the range these two articles set:

| Who violates the road-passage rules | Range set by the law |
|---|---|
| pedestrians, passengers, non-motor-vehicle drivers | a warning, or a fine of **five to fifty yuan** (Art. 89) |
| motor-vehicle drivers | a warning, or a fine of **twenty to two hundred yuan** (Art. 90) |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **So the question "how much is running a red light fined in province X" cannot be answered by the NPC Standing Committee.**
>
> The law gives **an interval**; where inside the interval it lands is **provincial legislation**.
> That is why the same violation can carry different amounts in different provinces —
> **and that reason appears on no ticket and on no sign.**

## Arguments

### A1 · The text itself allocates the authority (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: this is not a doctrinal gloss, it is **the statute's explicit allocation of powers**
- **Premise**: Article 123 gives the power to set "specific enforcement standards" to the provincial
  people's congress standing committees, bounded by "within the fine ranges prescribed by this Law" (ev-051)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. This is "**how the regime is laid down**" — exactly what the [law](../README.en.md) subject collects
  2. It is **falsifiable by the reader**: open the official page and search "第一百二十三条"
  3. The ranges themselves (5–50 in Art. 89, 20–200 in Art. 90) are equally explicit; only read
     together do the two articles form the complete two-layer structure
- **Evidence**: ev-051
- **Attacked by**: see A2
- **Signed**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-12

### A2 · This entry claims only one version (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: this entry claims "**the version amended on 2021-04-29 says so**", **not "it still holds now"**
- **Premise**: the official page carries **no revision number**, only a publication date and the
  three-amendment history (ev-051)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. the law **has already been amended three times** — this class of text **does get replaced**
  2. and this repo has **no mechanism** to detect a further amendment
  3. writing "still holds now" into the statement would be **beyond the evidence**
- **Evidence**: ev-051
- **Attacked by**: **this entry has not checked any single province's actual rules.**
  The law says provincial standing committees "**may**" set a standard inside the range —
  **whether a given province set one, and where inside the range it sits, this repo has not looked.**
  So this entry can only support "**to whom the authority is allocated**",
  **not "how much your province fines"**. Provincial rules are another layer of text, not yet registered here.
- **Signed**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-12
- **To fill in**: **the text of any one provincial standing committee's specific enforcement standard**.
  With it, this proposition can reach from "how the authority is allocated" to "what it actually is".

## Why this counts as real information rather than noise

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A ticket states the amount, but not who set it or why it is that amount.**
>
> What you can see on site is the **result** (a number);
> what the law writes down is the **structure** (ranges belong to the state, specific standards to the province).
>
> **This repo's mission is to strip noise and hold true information.**
> That contrast is the test: **what is visible on site, this repo need not repeat;
> what is invisible on site yet written in law is what belongs here.**
> "Why do the provinces fine differently" — the question has no answer on any sign.
