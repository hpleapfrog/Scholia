## Statement

《雅集科學新世紀 1A 冊》 (reprinted with corrections), unit 1–23, figure 1.21(b) uses

> a map of global earthquakes of magnitude 4+ from 1995–2001 (data source: China Earthquake Administration)

The publisher's errata list the **reason for the change** as: **"cite a reliable source"**,
with a reference link to the gov.cn earthquake-data page.

In other words: the data source the original map relied on was judged unreliable and must be replaced.

## Argument

### A1 · Publisher's statement (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the publisher states the map's data source is unreliable and must be replaced
- **Premise**: the Aristo official errata write "cite a reliable source" in the "reason for change" column, with a reference link (ev-015)
- **Reasoning**: the errata are published by the publisher, so the "reason for change" column is its official characterization of the problem
- **Evidence**: ev-015
- **Attacked**: none
- **Attribution**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-05

> [!NOTE]
> The errata **do not say** where exactly the original source is unreliable, nor give the new map's exact provenance —
> they only give the reason for change and one reference link.
> **Do not read "cite a reliable source" as a specific accusation against the original data.**

## Nature

This matters more than an ordinary erratum because what it involves is **data, not wording**:

| | ordinary erratum | this entry |
|---|---|---|
| what's wrong | wording, terminology, caption | **data source** |
| consequence | reader confusion | **readers form understanding on unreliable data** |
| detectability | often self-evident | **usually not self-evident** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This maps directly onto the evidence-grade principle in [evidence/README.md](../../../evidence/README.en.md):
> **the reliability of a data source is closer to an "evidence threshold" question than whether the wording is correct.**
> Data whose source cannot be traced must not enter the judgment, by the repo's standard.
