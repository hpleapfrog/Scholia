> **English version of [`动量守恒定律.md`](动量守恒定律.md).**

> [!TIP]
> **After reading this entry you should** be able to state under what conditions momentum is conserved,
> and to explain why conservation can only be discussed once "the whole system is taken into account".

## Definition

**The law of conservation of momentum**: in a system that **experiences no external forces** (or whose **net external force is zero**),
the system's **total momentum remains unchanged**.

```
m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁′ + m₂v₂′
```

- the keyword is **system** — not "one particular object"
- the term the textbook uses is **`isolated system`**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The textbook's `[AL]` hint requires special guarding against this point (ev-046):**
>
> > `Caution students that momentum is only conserved when the entire system affected`
> > `is taken into account. Explain isolated system.`
>
> **Then it gives a chain of follow-up questions**:
>
> > `Ask students to give examples of isolated systems.`
> > `Ask them if these are perfectly isolated.`
> > `Would it be possible to have perfectly isolated systems on Earth?`
>
> **The last question is the core of this section**: on Earth, perfectly isolated systems **almost never exist**.
> So "conservation of momentum" in reality always holds **under some approximation** —
> **this is not a discount on the law; it is the correct way to use it.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Before teaching this section, the textbook first asks about a word (ev-046):**
>
> > `Before students read the section, ask them what they understand by the word`
> > `conservation. Have they come across it in any other law of physics?`
>
> "Conservation" has already appeared in this repo before — [conservation of mechanical energy](机械能守恒.md) comes first.
> **The purpose of this question is to connect to it, not to treat it as a new word.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Its relation to [conservation of mechanical energy](机械能守恒.md) must be told apart, or students will mix them up.**
>
> | | Conservation of momentum | Conservation of mechanical energy |
> |---|---|---|
> | Quantity conserved | `p = mv` (**vector**) | `E = kinetic + potential` (**scalar**) |
> | Condition | the system **experiences no external forces** (or net external force is zero) | **only gravity/spring forces do work** (no friction, no non-conservative forces) |
> | In collisions | **usually holds** | **holds only in elastic collisions** |
>
> **"Momentum is conserved in collisions" is almost always true; "mechanical energy is conserved in collisions" is true only in elastic collisions.**
> The textbook covers this point in the next section ([elastic and inelastic collisions](弹性碰撞与非弹性碰撞.md)).

## Argument

### K23 · Why listed separately (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the law of conservation of momentum deserves to be a separate knowledge point
- **Premise**: the textbook devotes a section to it, containing **5 teaching hints** (2 of them tiered) and **10 terms** (ev-046)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. it is this chapter's **landing point** — [momentum and impulse](动量和冲量.md) is the tool, this is the conclusion
  2. it has a **premise that must be made clear** (the isolated system), and that premise **is always approximate in reality**; the textbook guards against this specifically
  3. it stands **alongside but does not overlap** the existing [conservation of mechanical energy](机械能守恒.md) — both the conserved quantity and the conditions differ
- **Evidence**: ev-046
- **Attacked**: **it has only 5 teaching hints and 430 characters of tiered content**, making it one of the **thinnest** nodes in this repo;
  and it connects directly to [momentum and impulse](动量和冲量.md) in the same chapter — **splitting them may sever the derivation chain**
- **Response**: do not merge. **The reason: "conservation" and "the definition of non-conservation" are two different things**:
  the former is a **tool** (`p`, `I`, `FΔt`), the latter is a **conditional conclusion** (when does the total stay unchanged).
  Merging would let the tool itself swallow the key point "under what conditions is it conserved".
  **But the thinness is real** — the `Definition` section of this entry writes only what the textbook explicitly states, **and adds no worked examples on its behalf**.
