> **English version of [`光的折射.md`](光的折射.md).**

> [!TIP]
> **After reading this entry you should** be able to compute the angle of incidence and the angle of refraction with n = c/v and the law of refraction, and to know that n is always greater than 1

## Definition

**Refraction**: when light passes from one medium into another, **its direction of propagation changes**.

- **refractive index** `n = c / v` — the speed of light in vacuum divided by its speed in the medium
- **law of refraction**: `n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂` (θ measured from the **normal**)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **`n` is always greater than 1; this can be derived, and students should not be made to memorize it (ev-038).**
>
> The speed of light in vacuum `c` is the **maximum value**, so in any medium `v < c` ⇒ `c / v > 1`.
>
> The approach in the textbook's `[BL]` hint is: **`c` is necessarily greater than `v` ⇒ `n` is necessarily greater than 1;
> demonstrate the division with numbers that divide evenly** to reinforce this point.
>
> `n` is a **dimensionless** number (the ratio of two speeds).

>
> **Answer: they should not be merged.** Three reasons:
>
> | # | Reason |
> |---|---|
> | 1 | **The two share the same prerequisite, and sharing a prerequisite is exactly what shows they are siblings.** The textbook **wrote the same `[BL]` hint once in each of the two modules, reflection and refraction**: "an angle is the measure of separation between two lines; **the length of the line segments does not matter**" (ev-026, ev-038). A prerequisite that gets **taught twice** means both nodes need it, not that one node was split into two |
> | 2 | **The two differ in nature, and the textbook explicitly contrasts this.** The `[OL]` original text: `unlike the law of reflection, the law of refraction is most easily expressed as an equation, rather than in words` (ev-038) — **reflection can be stated clearly in words, refraction cannot and needs an equation.** These are not two cases of the same thing |
> | 3 | **The two have different difficulties.** Reflection's difficulty is **geometry** (the normal, where the angle is measured from); refraction's difficulty additionally includes **inverse trigonometric functions** (`sin⁻¹` is not an exponent) and the physical meaning of `n` |
>
> **And the shared prerequisite is itself a new finding**: this repo currently **has no** prerequisite node for "the geometric angle",
> while **both entries say in their `禁忌` that they need it**. This is a recorded gap (see below).

## Argument

### K17 · Why listed separately (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: refraction of light deserves to be a separate knowledge point
- **Premise**: the textbook gives it a section, containing **10** teaching hints (6 of them tiered) and 9 terms (ev-038)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. it has **its own defining equation** (`n = c/v`) and **its own law** (`n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂`),
     and this law **can only be stated as an equation** (as the textbook itself points out)
  2. it has **its own mathematical difficulty**: inverse trigonometric functions. The textbook gives a dedicated `[OL]` hint explaining
     that the superscript in `sin⁻¹` **is not an exponent**
  3. it can explain a phenomenon that reflection cannot: **a prism splits white light into a rainbow, while window glass does not**
     (the textbook's `[AL]` hint asks for an explanation of this contrast)
- **Evidence**: ev-038
- **Attacked**: it is in the same chapter as [reflection of light](光的反射.md) and shares the same set of geometric prerequisites;
  **splitting them may lead students to treat "light meeting an interface" as two unrelated things**
- **Response**: do not merge (reasons above). **But the risk that attack points out is real** —
  so [reflection of light](光的反射.md) and this entry's `关联` **link to each other**, and both note that they are "sibling nodes".
- **Attribution**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-05
