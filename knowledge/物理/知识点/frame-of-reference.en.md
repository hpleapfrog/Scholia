## Definition

**A reference frame is the point of view on which a description of motion is based.**

The same motion can be described differently (even in opposite directions) under different reference frames.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A reference frame is **not** the "background" in which motion happens — this is the most common student misconception,
> see [misconception: taking a reference frame as background](../误解/frame-as-background.en.md).

## Argument

### K1 · Why listed separately as a knowledge point (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the reference frame deserves to be a separate knowledge point rather than merged into "speed and velocity"
- **Premise**: the textbook gives it its own section (OpenStax《Physics》§2.1, ev-021), and tags it with a dedicated Misconception
- **Reasoning**: the textbook has a standalone Misconception and three tiers of teaching methods, showing it has its own teaching goal and typical failure modes; merging it into an adjacent knowledge point would lose that structure
- **Evidence**: ev-021
- **Attacked**: a textbook's section division **is not** a cognitively independent unit — the division may just be for authoring convenience
