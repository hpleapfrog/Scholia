> **English version of [`万有引力定律.md`](万有引力定律.md).**

> [!TIP]
> **After reading this entry you should** be able to compute gravitational force with F = GmM/r², and distinguish G (a universal constant) from g (it varies with location)

## Definition

**Newton's law of universal gravitation**: any two particles attract each other with a force,

```
F = G · mM / r²
```

- `G` = **the universal gravitational constant**, `6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²`
- `m` and `M` are the two masses, `r` is **the distance between them**
- the direction lies along the line joining the two particles, **always attractive**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **G and g are two different things; the textbook explicitly names this confusion (ev-037).**
>
> | | `G` | `g` |
> |---|---|---|
> | what it is | **the universal gravitational constant** | **the acceleration due to gravity** |
> | does it vary | **the same everywhere** | **varies with location** (equator / poles / the Moon all differ) |
> | textbook wording | `G is a universal constant that applies anywhere in the universe` | `g is **not** a universal constant` |
>
> The textbook's `[OL]` hint is just one line: `Be sure no one is confusing G with g.`
> — **it treats this confusion as something that will happen by default.**

## Argument

### K16 · Why listed separately (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the law of universal gravitation deserves to be a separate knowledge point
- **Premise**: the textbook devotes a section to it, with **15 teaching hints** (8 of them tiered),
  5 terms, and a full set of worked examples (ev-037)
- **Reasoning**:
  1. it has **an independent formula** (`F = GmM/r²`) and **an independent constant** (`G`),
     and that constant has a confusion the textbook explicitly names for dispelling (`G` vs `g`)
  2. it lies **in the causal chain after Kepler's laws and before general relativity** —
     it is the **explanation** of the three laws, and at the same time the object **corrected** by relativity
  3. the textbook requires students to **explain** its difference from Kepler's work, which is an independent teaching task
- **Evidence**: ev-037
- **Attacked**: it is in the same chapter as and directly connected to [Kepler's laws](开普勒定律.en.md);
  **splitting it into two nodes may sever the main line "pattern → explanation"** —
  which is exactly the concern [Kepler's laws](开普勒定律.en.md) recorded itself in `K11`
- **Response**: **do not merge.** The reason is not "the textbook split it into two sections", but that **the two have different objects**:
  Kepler's is **observable** (orbits, periods), Newton's is **not directly observable** (force).
  Merging into one node would erase the distinction "which part is observation, which part is explanation" —
  and the textbook precisely requires teaching that distinction (see the `[OL]` hint above).

## What this node does not hold (recorded as is)

> [!WARNING]
> **There is still half a title in this module: `and Einstein's Theory of General Relativity`.**
>
> The textbook does give teaching content on relativity in this module (ev-037):
>
> | tier | key point of the wording |
> |---|---|
> | `[OL]` | have students arrange Kepler, Newton, and Einstein in **historical order** |
> | `[AL]` | distinguish special from general relativity: **special relativity is a theory of space and time** (applicable to uniformly moving observers), **general relativity is a theory of gravity** (applicable to accelerating observers) |
> | `[OL]` | have students **discuss this postulate** (the equivalence principle) and connect it to experience |
>
> **This has content; it is not "not enough teaching-method accumulation."**
>
> This repo **currently has no** "general relativity" node, and **no** "special relativity" node either.
> **This is a coverage gap on record** — not a judgment that "it should not be a node".
>
> **The reason for not building it, told honestly**: building a node that I consider important but **for which I have only this one source** would make this repo look broader than it actually covers. **Write the gap down here first.**
