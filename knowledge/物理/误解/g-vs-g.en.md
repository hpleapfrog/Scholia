> **English version of [`混淆G与g.md`](混淆G与g.md).**

## Statement

Students take `G` and `g` as the same thing, or think they are two spellings of one constant.

| | Student's impression | Actually |
|---|---|---|
| `G` | "gravity constant" | **gravitational constant**, `6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²`, **the same everywhere** |
| `g` | "gravitational acceleration" | **acceleration due to gravity**, about `9.8 m/s²`, **varies by location** |

The two differ by **eleven orders of magnitude**, have **different units**, and **one is universal, the other is not**.

## Why record it separately

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The textbook names it with an imperative, rather than explaining with it:**
>
> > `Be sure no one is confusing G with g.`
>
> (ev-037, `[OL]`)
>
> **The whole module has only this one sentence about the confusion.** The tone of "`Be sure no one is`" shows
> the textbook **expects it to have already happened**, so this is a **check item**, not an explanation to teach in advance.
>
> An `[AL]` hint reinforces the same thing from the other side:
> `Emphasize that g is not a universal constant.`

## Why students confuse them

- **The two symbols differ only in case**, and in physics case usually only distinguishes different **roles** of the same kind of quantity (e.g. `m` and `M`)
- In Chinese both carry "重" or "引力": **万有引力常数** / **重力加速度** — they sound like the same family
- Before meeting `G`, students have already done lots of free-fall calculations with `g` — **`g` is the familiar one**

## Frequency

> [!WARNING]
> **This entry has no quantitative data yet.**
>
> The existing basis only proves **the textbook lists it as something to check**,
> **not the occurrence rate** — the two are different; the former must not impersonate the latter.

## Argument

### M1 · The textbook names it as something to check (no attack yet)

- **Claim (narrowed)**: **the textbook's authors expect** this confusion to exist and consider it needs dedicated checking
- **Premise**: the textbook writes `Be sure no one is confusing G with g` in the `[OL]` level,
  and `Emphasize that g is not a universal constant` in the `[AL]` level (ev-037)
- **Reasoning**: the textbook names it with an imperative outside the main text, showing the authors expect default teaching not to hold it off
- **Evidence**: ev-037
- **Attacked**: **"the textbook names it" does not entail "students easily form it"** —
  writing the original wording as an occurrence rate would go beyond what the premise supports.
  The textbook's `Be sure no one is…` may also be **habitual teaching caution**, not based on measured frequency.
  This claim therefore **only declares the authors' expectation**.
