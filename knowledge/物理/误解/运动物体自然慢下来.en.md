> **English version of [`运动物体自然慢下来.md`](运动物体自然慢下来.md).**

## Statement

Students believe **a moving object slowing down needs no reason** — "it just stops on its own".

Two consequences, both serious:

1. **Directly conflicts with Newton's first law** — but students don't feel the contradiction, because everyday experience supports it
2. **Creates an "exception" to energy conservation** — a sliding object stops; where did the kinetic energy go?

## Basis

The textbook names this misconception explicitly when teaching energy conservation (short quotation):

> `Discuss the law of conservation of energy and dispel any misconceptions related to this law,
> such is the idea that moving objects just slow down naturally.
> Identify heat generated by friction as the usual explanation for apparent violations of the law.`

The same book handles the same thing from the other side in §4.2 on inertia (ev-025).

## Argument

### M1 · The textbook explicitly lists it as a misconception to dispel (**narrowed**)

- **Claim (current)**: **the textbook's authors expect** this misconception to exist and consider it needs dedicated dispelling
- **Claim (original, knocked down)**: ~~this is a understanding bias students **easily form** and that needs **dedicated intervention**~~
- **Premise**: the textbook, when teaching energy conservation, actively requires `dispel any misconceptions … such is the idea that moving objects just slow down naturally` (ev-028)
- **Reasoning**: the textbook uses the strong wording "dispel" outside the main text, showing the authors **expect default teaching not to hold it off**
- **Evidence**: ev-028
- **Attacked**:
  - **The original wording crossed from "authors expect" to "occurrence rate"** — and "what to do" does not entail "what actually happens".
    The textbook's original is an **imperative** (`Discuss… and dispel…`), and `such is the idea that…`
    is merely an **example** of `any misconceptions`, giving this misconception no separate weight.
  - **It contradicts this entry's `## Frequency`**: that section states in black and white
    "the existing basis … **does not prove the occurrence rate** — the two are different, the former must not impersonate the latter",
    while the original wording does exactly that impersonation.
  - Also: the original "evidence" column listed `ev-028, ev-025`, but both **belong to OpenStax《Physics》** —
    per `FORMAT.md` §6 **not independent evidence**. Narrowed to cite only ev-028.
- **Status**: **survives after narrowing** — the current wording is the one the premise truly supports
- **Attribution**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-05
- **To be added**: measured occurrence rate (FCI-style item distributions); a before/after comparison of dispelling

### M2 · "The student's observation is right, the attribution is wrong" (**knocked down**)

- **Original claim**: students see the real phenomenon (with friction, objects stop) but **attribute** friction's effect to a property of the object itself
- **Why it was written this way**: this claim is **not baseless** in the physics-education literature (some research writes the misconception's cause as "failing to identify friction as a force"). **But that is the literature's words, not this entry's evidence.**
- **Attacked (reason for knocking down)**:
  1. This is an empirical proposition about **the student's internal state**, and the evidence this entry holds
     is only two **textbook passages** — not a single word in the textbook measures student observation or attribution
  2. It **conflicts with this entry's `## Statement`**: the statement says students think slowing "**needs no reason**";
     while M2 says students **know** friction, just treat it as a property. **The two cannot describe the same batch of students at once**
- **Status**: **knocked down** (zero evidence)
- **To revive it needs**: a literature or data with a verifiable locator showing that students holding this misconception **actually**
  can describe friction and treat it as "a property of the object" rather than "a nonexistent cause"
- **Attribution**: original @hpleapfrog; knocked down by [the attack entry](../命题/攻击/攻击-运动物体自然慢下来.en.md#a2)

### M3 · "Correcting it **only** works by pointing out the ignored item" (**knocked down**)

- **Original claim**: the pedagogy for correcting this misconception is **unique**
- **Attacked (reason for knocking down)**:
  1. "**only**" is a universal claim about pedagogical **uniqueness**, and this entry has **zero comparison data** —
     neither the failure rate of the excluded practice (what exactly does "you observed wrong" mean in class?)
     nor the success rate of "point out friction"
  2. **Conflicts with another repo entry**: [机械能守恒 · OL：摩擦生热](../教法/机械能守恒-OL-摩擦生热.en.md)
     's `M1` explicitly writes "**'textbook-specified' ≠ 'measured effective'**; and there is no data on whether the misconception really disappears once named" —
     and "point out the ignored item" **is exactly** that teaching method. **The same method, treated as established here, admits no data there.**
- **Status**: **knocked down**
- **To revive it needs**: before/after dispelling comparison data; or downgrade "only" to "textbook-suggested" (that is the version with grounds)
- **Attribution**: original @hpleapfrog; knocked down by [the attack entry](../命题/攻击/攻击-运动物体自然慢下来.en.md#a3)
