> **English version of [`电流方向与电子流向混淆.md`](电流方向与电子流向混淆.md).**

## Statement

Students understand the **conventional direction of current** as **the direction electrons actually move**.

Consequence: systematic reversal when judging current direction and analyzing circuits; and because "the math gives the same result",
this error **does not show up in calculation problems** — it only goes wrong in the physical picture.

## Basis

| Number | Type | Grade | Locator |
|---|---|---|---|
| ev-018 | textbook (full text) | low | OpenStax《Physics》 Chapter 19 · Ohm's law, m54437, Teacher Support (CC BY 4.0) |

**Textbook original (short quotation)**:

> Make sure that students understand that current is defined as the direction in which positive charge
> would flow, **even if electrons are most often the mobile charge carriers**. Mathematically, the result
> is the same whether we assume positive charge flowing one way or negative charge flowing the opposite way.
> **Physically, however, the situation is quite different.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The textbook **writing this teaching hint of its own accord is itself evidence**: this is a place teachers must emphasize specifically, or students go wrong.
> This is not my inference.

## Why record it

> [!WARNING]
> This misconception is **extremely hidden**, for the reason the textbook itself gives:
> **"the math gives the same result"** — computing with positive or negative charge yields the same answer.
>
> So **a student who gets all calculation problems right may still have the physical picture backwards**.
> Ordinary tests cannot detect it; only direction-judgment or picture-explanation problems expose it.
