## Statement

Students understand a **reference frame** as "the **background** in which motion happens", not as "the **point of view from which motion is observed**".

Consequence: they treat the frame as an objectively existing scene rather than **the observer's choice**;
and so cannot grasp the **basis** for "the same motion is described differently in different frames".

## Basis

The textbook writes it directly with a **Misconception** tag in this section (short quotation):

> `Misconception: Students may assume that a reference frame is a background of motion
> instead of the frame from which motion is viewed.`

**And the textbook provides a matching demonstration**:

1. Have a student stand at the front of the classroom — they **represent "the background"**
2. The teacher **walks between** this student and the whole class
3. Ask the student and the class: **which way did the teacher move?**
4. The class says "right"; the student standing at the "background" position says "left"
5. Wrap up: **a reference frame is the observer's point of view, not the background**

## Why record it

> [!TIP]
> **This misconception comes with its own detection method.**
>
> The textbook's demonstration is not just "making it clear" — it is simultaneously a **diagnosis**:
> if a student still says "because the background is moving" after the demonstration, the misconception is not corrected.
>
> No paper test needed — **you can see on the spot who hasn't turned around**.
