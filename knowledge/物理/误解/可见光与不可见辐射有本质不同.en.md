## Statement

Students treat **visible light** as a different **kind** of thing from infrared, ultraviolet and X-rays,
rather than "the same phenomenon in different frequency bands".

Consequence: they understand one continuous **spectrum** as a **classification box holding several things**,
so they cannot understand the source of "different bands have different uses" (penetration), and can only memorize.

## Basis

The textbook, in the `[OL]` teaching hint, **explicitly requires dispelling** it (short quotation):

> `Dispel any misconceptions that visible light is somehow different from radiation we cannot see,
> except for frequency and wavelength.`

## Why record it separately

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The cause of this misconception is the senses, not ignorance.**
>
> People **can only see visible light**. So "what is seen is one kind, what is unseen another" is the **default classification the senses give** —
> the student is not wrong-thinking; **the senses provide no counterexample**.
>
> **So correcting it cannot rely on "explaining it again" — it needs evidence beyond the senses**:
> infrared felt as heat by skin, X-rays penetrating tissue, the greenhouse effect's difference between visible light and heat waves —
> all of these **make the invisible bands show up on another sense or an instrument**.
