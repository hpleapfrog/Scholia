## Argument

### A1 · "Carrying horizontally does no work (force perpendicular to displacement)" is wrong (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the **reason** of that criterion line does not stand, so the line as a **general rule** does not stand
- **Premise**: the original sentence makes "force perpendicular to displacement" the reason for the **whole situation**
- **Reasoning**:
  1. "perpendicular" holds only for the **vertical normal force**. But **the force that moves the box horizontally is in the same direction as the displacement**,
     **doing positive work** — if uniform, this work is all converted into friction heat.
  2. "carrying things" in the everyday sense includes **pushing and dragging**. When pushing a box the push does positive work;
     the original criterion **does not apply at all** to that situation.
  3. even counting only "carrying it in front while walking": during **start-up acceleration and stopping deceleration**,
     the horizontal component of the hand's force does positive work (ΔKE > 0).
  4. so "does no work" holds **at the process level** only under "uniform speed, no resistance, counting only vertical force";
     and the **reason** the entry gives is not that reason in any of the situations.
     **The correct reason is "the net work on the box = ΔKE = 0", and specifying which force.**
- **Evidence**: ev-036 (the textbook's original is `carrying something over a level surface is not work
  in the scientific sense` — **it gives no "perpendicular" reason**, that was added by this entry)
- **Attacked**: none
- **Strength**: **strong** (reviewed, accepted)
- **Attribution**: AI subagent proposed · @hpleapfrog reviewed · 2026-10-05

### A2 · "Muscle consumption is a physiological matter" is evasion, not argument (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: the original wrap-up replaced the only qualifying clause that could save the situation with a **disciplinary label**
- **Premise**: the original says "muscles do consume chemical energy (**muscle fibers repeatedly micro-contract**) …
  where the energy consumed inside the muscle goes is a **physiological matter**, not a counterexample to this criterion"
- **Reasoning**:
  1. "muscle fibers **repeatedly micro-contract**" is itself a description of "**there is force, there is displacement**" —
     per the entry's own `W = F·d`, **the work done by the muscle's internal force along its displacement is nonzero**.
  2. so the two sentences **are not contradictory** — but the original wrap-up **is not arguing they are not contradictory**:
     it uses "physiological matter" to move the counterexample away, and **what is moved away is precisely physics**.
  3. the correct wrap-up is the premise never written down: **work must specify "on which object, by which force"**.
     The work of external forces on the box is zero (true); the work of the muscle's internal force is nonzero and goes into internal energy (also true). **Both are physics.**
  4. assigning it to physiology also conflicts with the repo's own practice — [conservation of mechanical energy](../../知识点/conservation-of-mechanical-energy.en.md)
     precisely keeps the same kind of internal-force dissipation (friction heat) **inside physics**.
- **Evidence**: ev-036; [conservation of mechanical energy](../../知识点/conservation-of-mechanical-energy.en.md)
- **Attacked**: none
- **Strength**: **strong** (reviewed, accepted)
- **Attribution**: AI subagent proposed · @hpleapfrog reviewed · 2026-10-05

### A3 · "Tiring is not the same as doing work" is wrong as a general rule (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: that sentence needs a quantifier; the unbounded general rule conflicts with an in-repo counterexample
- **Reasoning**:
  1. if "tiring ≠ doing work" is used as a falsification rule, it must entail "tiring things do no work" —
     but **running is tiring, and running does work**.
  2. **this entry's own 教法 entry** already uses its "risk" column to patch this hole
     (preventing students from inferring "tiring things do no work").
     **An upstream general rule and a downstream patch show the general rule itself does not hold.**
  3. the correct statement is **neither sufficient nor necessary**: holding something still is tiring but does no work; free fall does work but is not tiring;
     running is both.
- **Evidence**: ev-036; [work and power · OL: does it count as work](../../教法/work-ol-what-counts.en.md)
- **Attacked**: none
- **Strength**: **medium** (reviewed, accepted)
- **Attribution**: AI subagent proposed · @hpleapfrog reviewed · 2026-10-05
