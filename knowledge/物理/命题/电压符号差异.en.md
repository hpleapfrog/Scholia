> **English version of [`电压符号差异.md`](电压符号差异.md).**

## Statement

For the same physical quantity (voltage / potential difference / 电压),

- Chinese textbooks use **`U`**: `U = I × R`
- English textbooks use **`V`**: `I = V / R`, `V = IR`

When a student switches from a Chinese textbook to an English one, that `U` and `V` denote the same quantity **is usually not stated explicitly**.

## Basis

| Number | Type | Grade | Locator | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ev-001 | textbook | low | 人教版《物理》九年级全一册, 2013, P.79 (uses `U`) | 2013 |
| ev-016 | textbook (full text) | low | OpenStax《College Physics 2e》§20.3 (uses `V`) | 2020 |

**Both are low-grade evidence, but this proposition's truth does not depend on grade** — it is merely a side-by-side statement of the fact "two textbooks used different symbols", checkable by reading the two texts.

## Argument

### A1 · Side-by-side texts (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: Chinese- and English-language textbooks use different symbols for the same physical quantity
- **Premise**: ev-001 uses `U`; ev-016 uses `V`
- **Reasoning**: two public texts directly compared suffice; no inference needed
- **Evidence**: ev-001, ev-016
- **Attacked**: none
- **Attribution**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-05

## Why this is worth recording

This is a **"notation trap"** rather than a physics-content error — each textbook's own notation is correct.

But it has real consequences for users: Chinese students get stuck on the formula when reading English textbooks, and this is **not a comprehension problem**.
