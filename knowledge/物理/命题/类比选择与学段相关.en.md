## Statement

Textbooks at different stages choose **different everyday analogies** when teaching "current / resistance":

| Textbook | Stage | Everyday analogy | Verification status |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenStax《Physics》 | **high school** | water flow / pipes / peas straw | ✅ full text read (ev-018) |
| OpenStax《College Physics 2e》 | **intro college** | friction / air resistance | ✅ full text read (ev-016) |
| 人教版《物理》九年级 | junior high | water pipe | ⚠️ **not verified** (see below) |

**Hypothesis**: the choice of everyday analogy is determined by **stage**, not by country or textbook —
lower stages use **intuitive analogies** (water flow), higher stages use **mechanistic analogies** (friction, collisions).

## Argument

### A1 · Two data points are consistent (**knocked down**)

- **Claim**: analogy style varies with stage
- **Premise**: ev-018 uses "water flow / pipe / peas"; ev-016 uses "friction / air resistance"
- **Reasoning**: the two books come from the **same publisher**, same subject, the only salient difference being stage ⇒ stage is the most likely independent variable
- **Evidence**: ev-018, ev-016
- **Attacked**: see below — **"the only salient difference is stage" does not hold**; this argument **has been knocked down**
- **Attribution**: @hpleapfrog · 2026-10-05

> [!CAUTION]
> **This argument has been knocked down by [proposition: with n=2 one cannot attribute among several equally varying variables](两个样本无法归因.en.md#a1).**
>
> What was hit is the phrase "the **only** salient difference is stage": the quantities that vary at once
> between the two books are at least **author team, series positioning, year of publication, abstraction demanded of the target reader**.
> At n=2, these explanations **fit the data equally well**, and the data alone cannot choose among them.
>
> **Note that the attack did not say "stage is irrelevant"** — it said **this evidence cannot support the attribution**.

### A2 · Is there a more fundamental variable, "proficiency"? (open)

- **Claim**: what actually matters may be not the stage but the **student's abstraction ability**
- **Reasoning**: stage is only a proxy for abstraction ability; if so, tiered teaching within the same stage should also change the analogy
- **Evidence**: (none for now)
- **Status**: **open**; needs data on the same batch of students across stages

## Why this is worth recording

It moves "teaching methods must coexist" from **"each has its own rationale"** to **"the coexistence has structure"**.

If the choice of analogy is stage-dependent, then the `conditions.prior_level` field of 教法 entries
is **not just a note but the main independent variable** — which directly affects:

- how 教法 entries should write their conditions of application
- how [paths](../README.en.md) should bind different teaching methods by stage in their arrangement

## How to falsify (all three are worth doing)

- [ ] find a **lower-stage** textbook that uses a friction analogy as its main analogy
- [ ] find a **college** textbook that uses a water-pipe analogy as its main analogy
- [ ] add a third-party stage (e.g. 人教版 high school) to rule out same-publisher origin
- [ ] verify whether 人教版 junior high really uses a water-pipe analogy (after replacing the example entry)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Sample n = 2 — this entry is only a hypothesis, not a conclusion.** It is recorded to mark the directions of verification,
> so that it is not read as an established fact.
