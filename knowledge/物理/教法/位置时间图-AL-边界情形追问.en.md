## Method

**After students have already read one graph**, use a chain of boundary cases to force them to face “the model is idealized”:

The textbook's three linked questions (short quotation):

> `Once the students have looked at and analyzed the graph, see if they can describe different
> scenarios in which the lines would be straight instead of curved? Where the lines would be
> discontinuous?`

> `Is it realistic to draw any position graph that starts at rest without some curve in it?
> Why might we be able to neglect the curve in some scenarios?`

One level higher (another `[AL]` in the same section): **the curve is “the slope of the slope”** — leading into the next chapter's acceleration.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| tier | **AL** (textbook marker `[AL]`) |
| prerequisite | can already read a straight line's slope = velocity; has analysed at least one graph |
| cognitive load | high — the load sits on **questioning the model's assumptions** (why that curve can be neglected) |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this one carries across tiers | **weak** — “the textbook did not specify” ≠ “not applicable”, it is merely **untested** |

> [!WARNING]
> **This entry has no population contraindication backed by strong evidence.**
>
> The textbook offers its suggestion only for this tier and **does not say the method is ineffective for other tiers**.
> Treating “the textbook did not mention it” as “not applicable” is **over-inference** — so it is honestly marked **weak**, nothing is invented.

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **introducing “the slope of the slope” too early** | the textbook puts acceleration in the **next chapter**. Here it is only a “preview”; expanding it pulls this chapter's goal off course | the textbook's `[AL]` in the same section words it `a preview of acceleration` |
| students taking “idealization” for “fake” | “that curve can be neglected” is not “the curve does not exist”. If this is not broken open, students slide into relativism | — |

> [!WARNING]
> This method is worth very little in a class that **has never handled real measurement**.
> The textbook's step rests on students **having already done the experiment and seen a real curve jitter** —
> jump straight to the philosophical questions and students will not be able to answer them.
