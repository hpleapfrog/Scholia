## Method

**Replace the abstract "measurement disturbs" account with an everyday scenario that can be demonstrated on the spot.**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Another model for explaining the uncertainty principle for students struggling with the concept:`
> `Imagine searching for a floating balloon in a dark room. When your hand strikes the balloon,`
> `you provide an impulse and move it from its original spot. While you learned the position of the`
> `object, you disturbed it during your search. As a result, it has a new momentum that is unknown!`

**The mapping of this analogy:**

| In the analogy | In physics |
|---|---|
| the dark room | the invisible **microscopic scale** |
| the hand reaching for the balloon | **the act of measurement** (you have to touch it to measure) |
| the moment you touch the balloon you push it away | the **impulse** given by the measurement changes the momentum |
| "position touched" yet "momentum unknown" | **position and momentum cannot both be determined** |

**Key point**: the textbook positions it as `Another model for ... students struggling with the concept` —
**this is a backup path for those who are stuck, not the main path.**

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **OL** (textbook tag `[OL]`) |
| Prerequisites | students have heard the standard statement but **have not accepted it** |
| Cognitive load | medium — the load sits in **replacing an abstract account with an imaginable action** |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who have not yet heard the standard statement | the textbook explicitly writes this is an **alternative path** for `struggling with the concept`; using it in place of the main path first would **bypass the standard statement** | **medium** — the textbook explicitly states the purpose |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this method works across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| **the analogy taken as the principle itself** | "uncertain because touching it pushes it away" is the **measurement-disturbance** picture. But the quantum uncertainty principle is **not** "the instrument is not good enough" — it does not disappear with better instruments. **This is the greatest danger of this analogy** |
| students then concluding "the particle really has a definite position, we just cannot measure it" | this is exactly the idea of the hidden-variables interpretation. **The textbook's analogy does not say this, but students will fill it in themselves** |
| swapping the balloon for a solid ball | the balloon's crucial features are **lightness and easy displacement**. Swap in a heavy object and the disturbance effect is gone; the analogy fails |

> [!WARNING]
> **The failure mode of this analogy is of the same kind as the [water-pipe analogy](water-pipe.en.md), but the consequence is heavier.**
>
> The water-pipe analogy going wrong merely skews students' understanding of the circuit mechanism; **this analogy going wrong makes students understand it as "quantum mechanics is just about not being able to measure"** —
> that is **a wrong conclusion about the nature of physics**, not a detail-level deviation.
>
> **The textbook is right to mark it a backup path; but when using it you must explicitly say "the analogy ends here".**
