## Method

The textbook uses the same three-part structure for **every section**:

| Segment | Its name in the textbook | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| ① opener | `Explore this Phenomenon` | gives a **concrete phenomenon** + **4–5 open questions**, no answer given first |
| ② main text | concepts and principles | explains the concepts (at junior high, qualitative is enough) |
| ③ return | `Putting It Together` | **goes back to the phenomenon from the opener** and answers it again with the new concepts |

**Example (Utah grade 7 §1.1, Forces and Motion)**: the opener is a photograph of a rolling-ball
fountain, asking "the ball isn't moving to begin with — how do you make it move?", "why does it keep
moving after you let go?", "without touching it, why does it slow down?"
— after the main text has covered force, mass, and balanced / unbalanced, the return segment asks
the same questions all over again.

**Key**: ① and ③ are **the same set of questions**. Segment ③ is not a review exercise; it is
**for students to discover that their own explanation has changed**.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Stage | junior high |
| Prerequisite | the textbook itself has a phenomenon page; **if it does not, the teacher has to create one** |
| Teacher's role | segment ① **does not explain**, it only collects what students say; only segment ③ closes it up |

## Contraindications

**Who or what situations does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| situations with no concrete phenomenon that can be presented | the structure depends on a concrete phenomenon you can return to repeatedly; abstract chapters have no counterpart | **medium** — inferred from the structure itself, not stated by the textbook |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **does not tier this**, so there is no information about use across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| **segment ① is skipped** | if the teacher explains the concepts straight away, the phenomenon page is just decoration, and segment ③ has nothing to return to |
| **segment ③ is omitted** | doing only ① and not ③, students get just an interesting hook; **the link between concept and phenomenon is never built** |
| segment ① hurries to give the answer | that segment's value lies in **exposing students' prior explanations**; once the teacher gives the answer first, it degenerates into an introduction |
| segment ③ is treated as a review exercise | segment ③'s purpose is to **compare how the explanation changed**, not to repeat from memory |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Whether this practice succeeds or fails lies almost entirely at "the two ends"**: how the middle
> text is taught hardly matters —
> omit either ① or ③ and the structure degenerates into ordinary lecturing.

## Argument

### M1 · The textbook adopts this structure section by section (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: this is a reusable teaching structure, and a public textbook adopts it section by section
- **Premise**: all 5 sections of chapter 1 of the Utah grade-7 textbook use `Explore this Phenomenon` → main text → `Putting It Together` (ev-024)
- **Reasoning**: the structure is used **systematically across the whole book**, not an arrangement preference of a few individual sections
- **Evidence**: ev-024
- **Attacked**:
  - **adoption by a textbook ≠ teaching effectiveness**; there is no effect size and no sample size
  - "used section by section" may equally be the inertia of an authoring template, not a pedagogical consideration
