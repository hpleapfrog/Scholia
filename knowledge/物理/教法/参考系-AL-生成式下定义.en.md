## Method

Hand the job of **defining** over **to the students**:

1. Have students describe the path of motion, stressing that **direction is a necessary component of the definition**
2. **Pair up**; each pair **writes its own definition of "motion"**
3. The whole class **compares and discusses** each group's definition
4. Follow up: which components **must** a definition of motion contain?

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Ask students to describe the path of movement and emphasize that direction is a necessary
> component of a definition of motion. Ask the students to form pairs, and ask each pair to come
> up with their own definition of motion. Then compare and discuss definitions as a class.
> What components are necessary for a definition of motion?`

There is also a deeper companion item (same section, `[AL]`): the reference frames in this chapter are
all **inertial reference frames** (not accelerating), and the key is **the difference in motion between
the observer's frame and the object's frame**.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **AL** (textbook marker `[AL]`) |
| Prerequisites | can construct definitions independently; can take part in peer review |
| Class organisation | needs a time budget for **pairing up + whole-class discussion** |
| Teacher's role | **converging** — does not lead, but must close on "direction is a necessary component" |

## Contraindications

**Who or what situations does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| situations that must be taught in a **non-inertial reference frame** | the textbook explicitly limits this chapter's reference frames to inertial frames (`they are not accelerating`); this practice **goes beyond that scope** | **strong** (stated by the textbook) |
| students outside this tier | the textbook gives **different practices** for BL / OL / AL, and does not declare this practice transferable across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| the definitions never converge | students' definitions are all over the place; if the teacher does not close on "direction is required", the lesson just stays in a divergent state | — |

> [!WARNING]
> **The failure mode is the opposite of the BL tier**: BL fears students **cannot get anything out**,
> AL fears students **say too much and cannot be reined in**.
> Same knowledge point, different tiers — the direction of the teacher's intervention is reversed.

## Argument

### M1 · Designated by the textbook for the AL tier (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: generative definition is the practice the textbook designates for advanced students
- **Premise**: the Teacher Support of m54108 marks it `[AL]` and gives the question above (ev-021)
- **Reasoning**: the tier marker is the textbook's designation for that level
- **Evidence**: ev-021
- **Attacked**: **"designated by the textbook" ≠ "measured to be effective"**; and this item also depends on class-organisation conditions (pairing + discussion time), costing more than the other two tiers
