## Method

### 1 · State the fact that “reflection can be stated clearly, refraction cannot”

> `Explain that, unlike the law of reflection, the law of refraction is most easily expressed`
> `as an equation, rather than in words.`

**The textbook makes this contrast on purpose.** It does two things:

1. **explains why the sine must appear here** — not that the teacher favours formulas, but that **this cannot be said in words**
2. **separates reflection from refraction** — they are **two laws of different character**, not two examples of one thing

### 2 · Walk it through with the lawnmower

> `Walk students through the lawnmower analogy in .`
> `Suggest other wheeled vehicles with which they may be more familiar, and other surfaces,`
> `such as sand.`

(The analogy given in the textbook's main text: a lawnmower drives **at an angle** from the concrete onto the grass;
the wheels on one side enter the grass first and slow down, and the whole machine **turns**.)

**The textbook requires pushing this analogy further**: have students swap in other wheeled things and other surfaces (such as sand).

### 3 · Nail down the notation most easily got wrong

> `The superscript in sin−1 is not a power. It indicates arcsine, which is an inverse`
> `trigonometric function. It means, "that angle whose sine equals (in this case) (n2/n1)."`

**The textbook devotes a whole hint to this one superscript.** And it gives a reading you can say out loud —
“**the angle whose sine equals `n₂/n₁`**”.

### 4 · Make clear the difference between sine and arcsine, and what “dimensionless” means

> `Explain the difference between sine and arcsine. Explain why sin 90° = 1.`
> `Note that the index of refraction is a dimensionless number.`

### 5 · One value worth memorising

> `Remind students that the maximum speed of light is its speed in a vacuum.`
> `This is a fundamental constant of physics. The maximum speed of light is equal to`
> `3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. Have your students memorize this value.`

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| tier | **OL** (textbook marker `[OL]`) |
| prerequisite | the law of reflection; the sine function; knowing the speed of light in a vacuum |
| teaching aid | none (the lawnmower is a verbal analogy and can be swapped for anything with wheels) |
| cognitive load | medium-high — **handling trigonometric notation and the physical law at the same time** |

## Contraindications

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **students who have not yet studied the sine function** | this entry's method is built on “what is sine”; the textbook needs a separate `[AL]` hint before it gives the definition of sine — **showing it is not assumed known** | **strong** — follows from the nature of the method |
| students who have not yet studied the law of reflection | the textbook's first sentence is `**unlike** the law of reflection` — **the contrast is this method's pivot** | **medium** (the textbook places reflection earlier in the same chapter) |
| students outside this (OL) tier | the textbook marks this paragraph `[OL]`; the BL and AL tiers get different suggestions | **medium** — the marker clearly sets the range, but “not marked” is still not “not applicable”, only **untested** |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| `sin⁻¹` taught as “sine to the power of minus one” | **this is the error the textbook guards against with a hint of its own.** Moreover `sin⁻¹(x)` and `1/sin(x)` give completely different values |
| the lawnmower analogy presented but never extended | the textbook explicitly asks to `Suggest other wheeled vehicles … and other surfaces` — **students are to generalise it themselves** |
| “refraction can only be stated as an equation” taught as “refraction is harder” | the textbook is talking about **the mode of expression**, not about difficulty. Reflection too has statements that can be made: three lines in one plane, equal angles |
| forgetting that `n` is dimensionless | `n = c/v` is one speed divided by another — students will want to attach a unit to it |
