## Method

**State the difference between “law” and “theory” explicitly — and point out that Kepler gave no explanation.**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Explain that Kepler's laws were laws and not theories. Laws describe patterns in nature that
> always repeat themselves under the same set of conditions. Theories provide an explanation for
> the patterns. **Kepler provided no explanation.**`

**Key point**: this is the sharpest sentence in the section, and also the one **most easily skipped**.

- **a law** = describes **recurring patterns**
- **a theory** = **provides an explanation** for the patterns
- Kepler gave the pattern; **the explanation came later, from Newton**

### The accompanying `[AL]` content

| Topic | What the textbook asks for |
|---|---|
| the demotion of Pluto | discuss it with the **centre-of-mass** criterion: for every planet–satellite system in the solar system the **centre of rotation lies inside the planet**; Pluto and Charon have similar masses, so the **centre of rotation lies in the space between the two** |
| eccentricity | give examples: **high eccentricity** (comets, Pluto) and **low eccentricity** (the Moon, Earth) |
| the ellipse formula | have students derive, from “string and drawing pin”, the relation between the major axis, the minor axis and the distance between the two foci — **a real engineering problem** (an elliptical table top and a mirror) |
| fractional powers | have students explain what the **fractional power on the right-hand side** means after solving `r³/T²` for T or r |

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| tier | **AL** (textbook marker `[AL]`) |
| prerequisite | able to separate “description” from “explanation”; able to handle fractional powers |
| cognitive load | high — the load sits on **a distinction at the level of philosophy of science**, not on computation |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students still building their ability to use formulas | this method lands on “the law/theory distinction” and **produces no problem-solving ability**; used at the wrong stage it misses on both counts | **medium** — inferred from the nature of the method |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this one carries across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| “a law is not a theory” heard as **“a law is inferior to a theory”** | the textbook means **a division of labour**, not a hierarchy. Once taught as rank, students' understanding of science gets worse instead |
| “Kepler gave no explanation” heard as **“Kepler's work was incomplete”** | this is **normal in the history of science**: the pattern comes first, the explanation after. The textbook immediately follows up with Newton, and its tone is neutral |
| the Pluto topic running away | the textbook's wording is `Prepare to discuss Pluto's demotion **if it comes up**` — **prepared for, not opened up on purpose** |
| the ellipse derivation turning into a pure algebra drill | the textbook points out that it is **a real engineering problem** (elliptical table tops, mirrors). Outside that context the motivation is lost |
