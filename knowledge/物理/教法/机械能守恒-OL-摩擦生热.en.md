## Method

**While teaching the conservation law, directly puncture the intuition that would make it "fail".**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Discuss the law of conservation of energy and dispel any misconceptions related to this law,
> such is the idea that moving objects just slow down naturally.
> Identify heat generated by friction as the usual explanation for apparent violations of the law.`

Steps:

1. teach the conservation law
2. **raise that counterexample proactively**: a sliding object comes to a stop "on its own" — doesn't that mean the energy is gone?
3. point out: **frictional heating** is the explanation — the energy did not disappear, it became heat
4. the same section's `[OL]` adds: when computing potential energy, **assume the velocity is constant**;
   the extra kinetic energy brought by the delivery velocity is lost when the object stops

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This step is not there to "add knowledge", but to dismantle an intuition that would make students abandon the conservation law.**
> Unless it is named, students will keep an exception in the back of their minds: "anyway, it just isn't conserved."

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **OL** (textbook tag `[OL]`) |
| Prerequisites | already knows that friction exists |
| Cognitive load | medium — the load sits in **replacing the old explanation with a new one** |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who do not yet know that friction exists, or have never seen frictional heating | the textbook uses frictional heating as the explanatory device; if students lack that concept, the replacement does not hold | **medium** — derived from the method |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this method works across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| remembering only the slogan "frictional heating" | students will treat friction as a universal patch, writing "turned into heat" whenever the destination of the energy is unclear |
| taking an "apparent violation" as a real violation | the textbook uses `apparent violations` — **it is "seemingly violates", not "violates"**. Once the wording loosens, the law gets taught as having exceptions |
| stopping at the macroscopic account | the textbook's `[AL]` gives the molecular-level explanation (macroscopic kinetic energy → atomic-scale kinetic energy). The OL tier does not need to rise to there |
