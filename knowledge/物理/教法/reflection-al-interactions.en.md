## Method

**Do not treat reflection as an isolated phenomenon; instead list how it combines with absorption and transmission.**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Recount and explain all the possible interactions of light with matter. Light can be absorbed
> at the surface of an opaque object. Some colors of light may be absorbed and others reflected.
> Light often is partially absorbed and partially reflected. It may also be transmitted through
> a transparent material, such as water or glass. Typically, if the surface of a transparent
> material is smooth, such as that of a window pane, light is transmitted partially and
> reflected partially.`

**Key**: **"partial reflection" is the norm**.
The textbook lets students see: reflection is not "happens or does not happen", but
**coexists with absorption and transmission in proportion**.

The same section's `[AL]` gives two more: the difference between a paraboloid and a spherical
surface (a short arc of a sphere can be approximated by a paraboloid); and solving a **reciprocal
equation** like `1/a = 1/b + 1/c` for any one of the variables.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **AL** (textbook marker `[AL]`) |
| Prerequisites | the law of reflection; can handle fractions and reciprocal equations |
| Cognitive load | high — the load is in **putting a single phenomenon back into the complete picture** |

## Contraindications

**Who or what situations does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who have not yet mastered the law of reflection itself | this practice assumes reflection is already established and only discusses how it combines with absorption / transmission | **medium** — inferred from the practice |
| students outside this tier | the textbook gives **different practices** for each tier, and does not declare this practice transferable across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| "partial reflection" gets taught as "reflection is inaccurate" | the textbook is talking about **energy being divided in proportion**, not measurement error. The confusion makes students think the law of reflection is only an approximation |
| the reciprocal equation becomes an extra math burden | the textbook lists this step separately under `[AL]`, showing that it treats it as **advanced content** that should not spill into lower tiers |
