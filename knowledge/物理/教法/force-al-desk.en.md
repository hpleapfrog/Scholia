## Method

The textbook's `[AL]` hint **takes three steps with the same desk**, adding one concept at each step.

### Step 1 · One person pushes and the desk moves; two push against each other and it does not

> `Ask students what would happen if more than one force is applied to an object. Take a heavy`
> `object such as a desk for demonstration. Ask one student to push it from one side.`
> `Explain how force and motion work. Now ask a second student to push it in the opposite`
> `direction. Ask students why no motion occurs, even though the first student applies the`
> `same amount of force. Introduce the concept of adding forces.`

**The key is that final follow-up question**:

> **"The force the first student uses hasn't changed at all — why doesn't the desk move?"**

**Students must separate "force" from "net force"** to answer it. The textbook uses no formula —
it uses this desk.

### Step 2 · Make magnitude and direction explicit

> `Explain that both magnitude and direction must be considered when talking about forces.`
> `By using physical objects, demonstrate how different forces acting together can be additive`
> `if they act in the same direction or cancel one another if they act in opposite directions.`

**The two sentences give two rules in order**:

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| same direction | **add** |
| opposite directions | **cancel** |

> `Explain the terms acting on and being acted on.`

**This sentence is laid down for the third law**: "A **acts on** B" and "B **is acted on** by A"
are two ways of saying the same interaction.

### Step 3 · Put the force diagram on paper

> `Ask students to give everyday examples of situations where multiple forces act together.`
> `Draw free-body diagrams for some of these situations.`

**The textbook requires students to give their own examples first, then draw force diagrams** —
note the order: **the real situation comes first, the diagram after**, not teaching the rules of
drawing first.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **AL** (textbook marker `[AL]`) |
| Prerequisites | vectors and their representation (the textbook asks for a review of them at the `[BL]` tier) |
| Teaching aids | **one heavy desk** + two students (specified by the textbook); paper and pen for drawing |
| Cognitive load | medium-high — **handling "two forces" and "direction" as two layers at once** |

## Contraindications

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **students who have not yet met vectors** | step 2 is built entirely on "force has direction"; the textbook puts the vector review at the `[BL]` tier, showing **it is not a prerequisite of AL but something earlier** | **strong** (the textbook tiered it) |
| a classroom with no heavy object to push, or too few people | the textbook's demonstration **needs two people pushing against each other on the same desk** — this is a practice **with physical preconditions** | **medium** — inferred from the practice itself |
| students **outside this tier** (AL) | the textbook marks exactly this passage `[AL]`; the OL and BL tiers get different suggestions | **medium** — the marker explicitly delimits the range, but "not marked" still does not mean "does not apply", only **untested** |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| the desk not moving gets explained as "the forces cancelled out, so there is no force" | **both forces are still there**; what cancels is their **combined effect**. That is exactly what step 1's follow-up question is there to prevent |
| teaching the rules for drawing force diagrams directly | the textbook lands on `Ask students to give everyday examples` — **examples first, then the diagram**. Reversing it turns the force diagram into a set of notations |
| `acting on` / `being acted on` gets glossed over | the textbook lists this sentence specifically, and **the third law is built entirely on it** |
| two people pushing the desk turns into a game | the textbook's step 1 is **first one person pushes** (the desk moves), **then two push against each other** (it does not move). Without the first half there is no contrast |

## Argument

### M1 · Designated by the textbook for the AL tier (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: this is the practice the textbook designates for advanced students
- **Premise**: the Teacher Support of m54135 marks the content above `[AL]` (ev-041)
- **Reasoning**: the tier marker is the textbook's designation for that passage
- **Evidence**: ev-041
- **Attacked**: **"textbook-designated" ≠ "measured to be effective"**;
  and this item depends on **a heavy desk** — **in a classroom with different conditions (or without
  a second student to cooperate) it cannot be done**, a point the textbook does not mention.
  It is an instance of what this repo's [coverage comparison](../覆盖度/README.md) records as
  "no data on the transferability of teaching methods"
