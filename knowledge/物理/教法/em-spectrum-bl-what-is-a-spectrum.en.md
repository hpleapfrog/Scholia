## Method

**Explain what a "spectrum" is first, then the electromagnetic spectrum.**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Explain that the term spectrum refers to a physical property that has a broad range with values
> that are continuous in some cases and, in other cases, discrete.
> Ask for other examples of spectra, for example, sound, people's heights, etc.`

Steps:

1. **a spectrum = a physical property with a broad range of values** (continuous in some cases, discrete in others)
2. have students give other examples: **sound**, **people's heights**…
3. only then move on to the electromagnetic spectrum

The accompanying review (textbook, same section, `[BL]`):

> `Review wave properties: frequency, wavelength, and amplitude. Ask students to recall sound and
> water waves, and explain how they relate to these properties.`

And one data observation (same section, `[BL]`):

> `Notice that most harmful forms of EM radiation are on the high-frequency end of the spectrum.`

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **BL** (textbook tag `[BL]`) |
| Prerequisites | **frequency / wavelength / amplitude** — the textbook explicitly requires reviewing them first |
| Cognitive load | low — the load sits in **concretizing an abstract concept** (sound, heights) |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who have not yet mastered frequency, wavelength and amplitude | the textbook places "review wave properties" alongside this method under `[BL]`, showing it treats this as a prerequisite | **medium** — the textbook explicitly requires a review |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this method works across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| the "people's heights" example breeding a **discrete / continuous confusion** | the textbook says "continuous in some cases, discrete in others", while height is an approximately continuous quantity. Unless this is pointed out, the example creates a new problem instead |
| "spectrum" explained for too long | it is a **prerequisite concept**, not the goal of this section. The textbook puts it in the first step to build the road, not to linger |
| "the high-frequency end is harmful" memorized as a conclusion | the textbook's wording is `Notice` (observation), not "high frequency therefore harmful". **The causal chain is left to the later penetrability** |
