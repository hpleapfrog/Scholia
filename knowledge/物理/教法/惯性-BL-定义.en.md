## Method

**Review Newton's first law first, then directly give the property its name.**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Review Newton's first law. Explain that the property of objects to maintain their state of motion is called inertia.`

Three steps:

1. review the first law (an object with no external force acting on it remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion)
2. point out the property of **objects maintaining their existing state of motion**
3. name this property: **inertia**

**Key point**: no reasoning chain is given, students are not left to guess. The BL tier wants **a memorable formulation**;
the later question of "what does inertia depend on" is handed to the AL tier.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **BL** (textbook tag `[BL]`) |
| Prerequisites | already knows Newton's first law |
| Cognitive load | low — the load sits in **naming and memorizing**, not in reasoning |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who have not yet encountered Newton's first law | the first step of this method is "review the first law"; without it there is nothing to name | **medium** — derived from the steps of the method |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this method works across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| giving only the name without explaining what "maintain state of motion" means | students remember the word "inertia" but cannot connect it to the first law, and the naming falls flat |
| being taken as "the reason objects stop by themselves" | everyday experience is precisely the opposite. Without drawing the distinction, students may understand inertia as "the thing that makes objects stop" — **the direction is exactly reversed** (see [related misconception](../误解/运动物体自然慢下来.en.md)) |
