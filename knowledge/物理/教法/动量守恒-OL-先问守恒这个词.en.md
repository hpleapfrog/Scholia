## Method

The textbook's `[OL]` hint for this section **is one sentence long, and it teaches nothing new.**

> `Before students read the section, ask them what they understand by the word conservation.`
> `Have they come across it in any other law of physics?`

### Why it is done "before reading"

**Before students read this section, they have already met "conservation"** — this very repo has a
[Conservation of mechanical energy](../知识点/机械能守恒.en.md) node, earlier in this chapter.

**What the textbook wants is to call the old word back out, not to give a new definition.** The order is:

| Step | Who acts |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Students say**: what "conservation" means |
| 2 | **Students find**: which other law of physics contains it |
| 3 | The teacher picks up: so are conservation of momentum and conservation of mechanical energy **the same thing** |

**Step 3 is where this question really lands.** And **the teacher cannot ask it first** —
ask it first and it becomes "today we cover the difference between two conservations",
and the students' old experience is no longer a resource.

### What it leaves out

**Not a single definition is given.** The textbook does not write "conservation means a quantity stays unchanged through a process";
it assumes students **can state the meaning from the two or three examples they already have**.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **OL** (textbook tag `[OL]`) |
| Prerequisites | **has already studied at least one other conservation law** (this repo has [Conservation of mechanical energy](../知识点/机械能守恒.en.md)) |
| Duration | 3–5 minutes, **must be before reading the main text** |
| Cognitive load | low — **it introduces nothing new** |

## Contraindications

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students **who have not yet studied any conservation law** | the textbook's second sentence is `Have they come across it in any other law of physics?` — **this is the entire basis of the hint**; with no old experience it falls flat | **strong** — follows from the nature of the method |
| students who have already read the main text | the textbook explicitly writes `**Before** students read the section` — **the order is part of the hint** | **strong** (the textbook explicitly states `Before`) |
| students **outside** this tier (OL) | the textbook tags exactly that passage `[OL]`; the `[AL]` tier gets a separate hint about "isolated systems" | **medium** — the tag clearly draws the scope, but "not tagged" still ≠ "does not apply", it is merely **untested** |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| the teacher gives the definition of "conservation" first | **then the students have nothing to do.** All the force of this question lies in "let them say it themselves" |
| asking only "what does it mean", not "where else have you seen it" | the textbook has **two sentences**. The second is the step that calls the old knowledge back out |
| placed after reading the main text | the textbook explicitly writes `Before`. After reading, "conservation" already looks to students like **the word of the momentum section**, no longer **a word they already own** |
| Step 3 turning into "today we cover the difference between two conservations" | that again gives the landing point away in advance. **The difference must be drawn out of the students' answers, not announced by the teacher** |

## Argument

### M1 · Designated by the textbook for the OL tier (no attack yet)

- **Claim**: this is the method the textbook specifies for on-grade-level students
- **Premise**: the Teacher Support of m54202 tags the two sentences above with `[OL]` (ev-046)
- **Reasoning**: a tier tag is the textbook's designation for that passage
- **Evidence**: ev-046
- **Attacked**: **"the textbook specifies it" ≠ "measured to be effective"**;
  more fundamentally — **the textbook only gives two sentences, while this entry writes out "why these three steps" and "where step 3 lands";
  that layer is this repo's interpretation, not the textbook's own text.**
- **Response**: in `Method` this entry **marks sentence by sentence which words are the textbook's own and which are this repo's expansion** (see the section "What it leaves out").
