## Method

**Do not start from the coordinate axes; start from a story.**

The narrative the textbook gives: launch a water rocket into the air, it rises to 150 ft, pauses, then falls back to the ground. Then ask in turn:

1. **where do you put the zero**?
2. **which way is the positive direction**? And the negative?
3. have one student **draw the scenario on the board**
4. then lead the whole class in drawing the **position-time graph**
5. follow up: is the line straight? Is it curved? Does it change direction? **what can you read off the graph**?

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Describe a scenario, for example, in which you launch a water rocket into the air.
> It goes up 150 ft, stops, and then falls back to the earth. Have the students assess the situation.
> Where would they put their zero? ... Have a student draw a picture of the scenario on the board.
> Then draw a position vs. time graph describing the motion.`

**The order is key**: draw **the scenario** first, then **the graph**. The scenario picture is the bridge between the graph and the physics.

| students **outside** this tier (BL / OL) | the textbook **tags exactly those two tiers for this passage**; the AL tier gets different suggestions | **medium** — the tag clearly draws the scope, but "not tagged" still ≠ "does not apply", it is merely **untested** |

> [!WARNING]
> **This entry has no population contraindications backed by strong evidence.**
>
> The textbook only gives suggestions for this tier, **it does not say this method is ineffective for other tiers**.
> Treating "the textbook did not mention it" as "does not apply" is **over-inference** — here it is honestly marked **weak**, nothing is fabricated.

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **students think the choice of zero does not affect the graph** | the textbook specifically follows up in the same section's `[OL]`: `Ask if the place that they take as zero affects the graph.` — showing this is a place that must be named | textbook, same section `[OL]` |
| skipping "draw the scenario" and drawing the graph directly | the textbook's order is **scenario first, graph second**. Hand over the coordinate axes directly and students lose the physical thing to correspond to | — |

> [!NOTE]
> The textbook puts "whether the zero affects the graph" under `[OL]` rather than `[BL]` — **showing it judges this step on the hard side for the BL tier**.
> When using it, this step should be cut or deferred according to the level.
