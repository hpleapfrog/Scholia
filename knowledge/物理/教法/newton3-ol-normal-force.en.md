## Method

### 1 · First review the first two laws

> `Review Newton's first and second laws.`

**This is the first sentence of this section's `[OL]`.** The textbook does not teach the three laws as three independent things —
**the third has to come after the first two.**

### 2 · Review “weight is a force”

> `Review the concept of weight as a force.`

(The textbook marks this one `[BL]` — **it is earlier material**; the OL tier only needs it touched on.)

### 3 · Use a falling question to bring in the support force

> `Ask students what happens when an object is dropped from a height.`
> `Why does it stop when it hits the ground?`
> `Introduce the term normal force.`

**These three sentences are one complete teaching move**:

| Sentence | What it is doing |
|---|---|
| 1 | gives a phenomenon **every student has seen** |
| 2 | asks a question **they cannot answer** |
| 3 | supplies the term: **support force** |

**Note what the second sentence asks**: “why does it **stop**” —
if a student answers “because the ground blocked it”, pressing on reveals that “blocking” **requires a force**,
and that force is the support force. **The textbook does not explain; it only gives the name.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The core of the third law is hidden inside this method.**
>
> The object presses the ground (object on ground), the ground supports the object (ground on object) —
> **these are two forces, acting on two different objects**.
>
> The textbook **does not say “this is Newton's third law” here**; it only gives the name “support force”.
> **The name lands first; the law arrives later.**

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| tier | **OL** (textbook marker `[OL]`) |
| prerequisite | Newton's first and second laws (the textbook requires reviewing them); the concept “weight is a force” |
| cognitive load | low — **a single sentence introduces one new term** |

## Contraindications

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who **have not yet studied the first and second laws** | the **first sentence of this hint is a review of them** — there is nothing to review | **strong** — inferred from the nature of the method |
| students who still take weight to be “how many kilograms” | the textbook puts `weight as a force` at `[BL]` — **it is an earlier prerequisite** | **medium** (the textbook tiered it) |
| students outside this (OL) tier | the textbook marks this paragraph `[OL]`; the AL tier gets different suggestions | **medium** — the marker clearly sets the range, but “not marked” is still not “not applicable”, only **untested** |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| stopping at “the ground blocked it” | after that step you **must get to “blocking requires a force”**, otherwise the support force is just one more term to memorise |
| formally teaching the third law right here | the textbook **only gives the name**. It puts the formal discussion of action and reaction forces later (see another teaching method) — **this is groundwork, not the landing point** |
| teaching the support force as “equal to gravity” | “equal” holds **in equilibrium**, whereas **the support force and gravity are not an action–reaction pair at all** (they act on **the same object**). **This is the most easily mis-taught point in this section** |
| skimming over the review of the first two laws | the textbook puts **the single `[OL]` review sentence** at the very front. Skip it and this section's OL method is nothing but a term |
