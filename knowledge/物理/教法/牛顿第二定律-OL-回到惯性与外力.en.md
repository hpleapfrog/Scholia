## Method

This section's `[OL]` hint is **only one item**, and what it does is **go back and teach the first law**.

> `Review the concepts of inertia and Newton's first law. Explain that, according to`
> `Newton's first law, a change in motion is caused by an external force.`
> `For instance, a ball that is pitched changes its speed and direction when it is hit by a bat.`

## Why this step is design, not groundwork

| What the first law says | What the second law says |
|---|---|
| with no **external force**, the state of motion **does not change** | when there is an external force, **how much it changes** is given by `F_net = ma` |

**The first law handles "whether or not"; the second law handles "how much".**
Unless "change is caused by an external force" is made clear first, `F_net = ma` is just an
equation waiting to be memorized.

**The example the textbook gives is deliberate**: a **pitched ball** travels along its own
direction, and only when **hit by a bat** does it change speed and direction —
**"external force" and "change" appear in the same sentence**.

## Contraindications

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **students who have not yet studied inertia** | this hint **is entirely "review inertia and the first law"** — there is nothing to review | **strong** — inferred from the nature of the practice |
| students who already solve problems fluently with `F = ma` and just want more practice | this practice **produces no new problem-solving ability** | **medium** |
| students **outside this tier** (OL) | the textbook marks exactly this passage `[OL]`; the AL tier gets a different suggestion | **medium** — the marker explicitly delimits the range, but "not marked" still does not mean "does not apply", only **untested** |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| reviewing the first law takes too long | in this hint it has room for only two sentences; **it lands on bringing in "external force"**, not on re-teaching the first law |
| the bat example gets taught as "the ball is acted on, so the ball moves" | the ball's **speed and direction both changed** — what the textbook uses it to show is **change**, not "motion" |
| treating the "review" as something skippable | the textbook spends its **only** `[OL]` hint on the review. Skip it and this section has no `[OL]` practice at all |
