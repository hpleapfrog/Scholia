## Method

**Do not give the conclusions; let students read them out of the observations themselves.**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Students should recognize two distinct results from the gold foil experiment.`
> `(1) The gold nucleus is very dense. (2) The atom is mostly empty space.`
> `Have students identify what evidence from the gold foil experiment supports those two statements.`

Steps:

1. First lay out **the two conclusions** (the nucleus is very dense / the atom is mostly empty)
2. Then have students **go back and point to** which **observations** in the gold-foil experiment support each one

**The two conclusions correspond to two oppositely directed parts of one and the same set of observations:**

| Conclusion | Observation |
|---|---|
| the nucleus is extremely dense | **a minority** of α particles **bounce back at large angles** |
| the atom is mostly empty | **the vast majority** of α particles **pass through with almost no deflection** |

**Key point**: the value of this step is — **looking only at "most pass through" misses all the information carried by "a minority bounce back"**.
Conversely, looking only at "a minority bounce back" will make students picture the atom as solid.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **OL** (textbook tag `[OL]`) |
| Prerequisites | has already seen the observations of the gold-foil experiment (no need to know the conclusions first) |
| Cognitive load | medium — the load sits in **the two-way matching of observation and conclusion** |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who have not yet seen the observations of the experiment | the action of this method is "go back and point to the observations"; with no observations to point at, it cannot proceed | **medium** — follows from the method |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this method works across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| having students only memorize the two conclusions | the textbook's required action is "**point to the evidence**". Skip that step and the two conclusions degenerate into two sentences to memorize |
| describing the "large-angle bounce" as a **direct** observation of "hitting the nucleus" | the experiment **did not** see the nucleus directly. The bounce is the basis of an **inference**, not a photograph of the nucleus. If this step is vague, students will think the nucleus was seen |
| ignoring that "mostly empty" also needs evidence | it rests on **the vast majority of particles passing straight through**, which matters just as much as the "bouncing back" |
