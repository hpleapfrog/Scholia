> **English version of [`波-BL-脉冲与周期.md`](波-BL-脉冲与周期.md).**

## Method

**Open up the classification along both dimensions in one go, so that students do not memorise the classification as mutually exclusive boxes.**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Any kind of wave, whether mechanical or nonmechanical, or transverse or longitudinal,
> can be in the form of a pulse wave or a periodic wave.`

**Key point**: the information in this sentence sits on the word “**any**” —

| Classification dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| whether a medium is required | mechanical wave · nonmechanical wave |
| direction of vibration vs direction of propagation | transverse wave · longitudinal wave |
| **form of disturbance** | **pulse wave · periodic wave** |

**The first two groups are “what it is”, the third is “how it is disturbed” — the three dimensions can be combined freely.**

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| tier | **BL** (textbook marker `[BL]`) |
| prerequisite | none (this method is itself the classification framework) |
| cognitive load | low — the load sits on **the three dimensions not being mutually exclusive** |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students with no concrete experience of a wave yet | a classification framework only stands up when hung on examples | **medium** — inferred from the nature of the method |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this one carries across tiers | **weak** — untested |

> [!WARNING]
> **This entry has no population contraindication backed by strong evidence.**
>
> The textbook offers its suggestion only for this tier and **does not say the method is ineffective for other tiers**.
> Treating “the textbook did not mention it” as “not applicable” is **over-inference** — so it is honestly marked **weak**, nothing is invented.

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| **treating the three dimensions as mutually exclusive classifications** | this is exactly what the method guards against. The textbook uses the “any … can be …” construction, showing that it expects students to **string the classifications into a tree** |
| memorising the classification names one by one | memorising is not the same as understanding “freely combinable”. Have students **give an instance of a combination** (e.g. nonmechanical + transverse + periodic = light) |
