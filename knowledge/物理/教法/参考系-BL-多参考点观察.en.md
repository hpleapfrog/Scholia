> **English version of [`参考系-BL-多参考点观察.md`](参考系-BL-多参考点观察.md).**

## Method

**Do not give a definition first**; instead, have students describe the same motion from several positions:

1. Push a book from one end of the desk to the other
2. Have different students describe its direction of motion from **their own position**, from **the book's position**, and from **another student's position**
3. The three descriptions contradict each other → this leads to "describing motion requires saying first where you look from"

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `slide a book across a desk. Ask students to describe its motion from their reference point,
> from the book's reference point, and from another student's reference point.`

**Key point**: the contradiction is **manufactured on purpose**. Students are not told "a reference frame is needed" — they **run into** that need themselves.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **BL** (textbook tag `[BL]`) |
| Prerequisites | can describe direction and position; **no** concept of coordinates or vectors **required** |
| Equipment | a single book is enough |
| Cognitive load | low — the load sits in **observing and expressing**, not in symbols |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each of** BL / OL / AL and does not state that this method works across tiers | **weak** — "the textbook does not specify it" ≠ "it does not apply", it is merely **untested** |

> [!WARNING]
> **This entry has no population-level contraindication supported by strong evidence.**
>
> The textbook only offers advice for this tier and **does not say this method is ineffective for other tiers**.
> Treating "the textbook did not mention it" as "it does not apply" is **over-inference** — here it is honestly marked **weak**, not invented.

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **students take a reference frame to be the "background"** | the textbook explicitly lists this misconception: `Students may assume that a reference frame is a background of motion instead of the frame from which motion is viewed` | the Misconception note in the same section of the textbook |
| demonstrating only, without naming the point | the three inconsistent descriptions **will not on their own** produce the concept of the "reference frame"; the teacher must explicitly close the loop | — |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The textbook does **not** state that this method is unsuitable for OL / AL students — for those levels it **gives a different method**.
> **"no contraindication recorded" is not the same as "confirmed to be applicable"**; the two must not be confused.
