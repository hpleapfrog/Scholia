## Method

**Both prerequisites must be established first, otherwise the formula that follows has nowhere to stand.**

### Prerequisite 1: the meaning of an angle (from geometry)

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Recall that, in geometry, angles are numbers that tell how far two straight lines are spread apart.
> The lines must be straight lines for the number to have meaning.`

### Prerequisite 2: "light bounces back" is a simplification

> `Explain that light bounces is a simplification. The geometry of the path of a bouncing ball is similar
> to that of light, but what happens at the point of impact is different at the molecular level.`

**Key point**: the second prerequisite **is not supplementary commentary, it is error-proofing**.
The everyday phrase "bouncing" gives a **macroscopic geometric** similarity while the mechanism is entirely different —
if this is not named, students will understand the reflection of light as a ball hitting a wall.

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| Level tier | **BL** (textbook tag `[BL]`) |
| Prerequisites | the geometric concept of **"angle"** — confirm this first, then teach reflection |
| Cognitive load | low — the load sits in **clarifying an existing concept**, not in a new formula |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who have not yet grasped the geometric meaning of "angle" | the textbook explicitly says `The lines must be straight lines for the number to have meaning` — **when the precondition fails, neither the angle of incidence nor the angle of reflection means anything** | **strong** (stated explicitly by the textbook) |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this method works across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| treating "'bouncing' is a simplification" as "'bouncing' is wrong" | the textbook says **geometry similar, mechanism different**, not that bouncing is entirely wrong. Over-correcting will make students discard the geometric picture along with it |
| skipping the geometric prerequisite and going straight to the formula | the textbook puts it first, which shows the writers consider it a common breaking point |
