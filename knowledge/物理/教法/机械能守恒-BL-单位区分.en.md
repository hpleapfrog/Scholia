## Method

**First make the dimensional relations of the three concepts clear, then talk about conservation.**

Textbook original (short quotation):

> `Make it clear that energy is a different property with different units than either force or power.`

> `Be sure there is a clear understanding of the distinction between kinetic and potential energy and
> between velocity and acceleration. Explain that the word potential means that the energy is available
> but it does not mean that it has to be used or will be used.`

**Four pairs to distinguish**:

| Easily confused | What must be distinguished |
|---|---|
| energy / force / power | **three different dimensions, different units** |
| kinetic / potential energy | kinetic looks at motion, potential looks at position |
| velocity / acceleration | one describes motion, the other describes change |
| potential energy “existing” / “being used” | `potential` only means **available**, not **will be used** |

## Conditions of use

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| tier | **BL** (textbook marker `[BL]`) |
| prerequisite | none (this method is itself prerequisite clarification) |
| cognitive load | low — the load sits on **conceptual distinction**, not on computation |

## Contraindications

**Who does it not apply to?**

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who have not yet studied power | the textbook requires distinguishing “energy / force / power”; before power has appeared, that distinction cannot be set up | **medium** — inferred from the method |
| students outside this tier | the textbook **gives a different method for each tier** and does not state that this one carries across tiers | **weak** — untested |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| “potential energy is only available, not necessarily used” taught as “potential energy may not exist” | the textbook is talking about **whether it is used**, not **whether it exists**. The confusion makes students think potential energy is probabilistic |
| turning the conceptual distinctions into memorising terminology | without examples the four pairs degenerate into a pile of definitions. The textbook puts them at `[BL]` precisely because this is **the most basic confusion point** |
