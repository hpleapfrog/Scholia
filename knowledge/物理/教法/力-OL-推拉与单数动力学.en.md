## Method

### 1 · Start from a fact everyone has seen

> `Point out that objects at rest tend to stay at rest. A ball, for example, moves only when`
> `pushed or pulled. The action of pushing or pulling is the application of force.`
> `Force applied to an object changes its motion.`

**Four sentences complete one chain**:

| Sentence | What it says |
|---|---|
| 1 | objects at rest **tend to stay at rest** |
| 2 | a ball **moves only when pushed or pulled** |
| 3 | **pushing or pulling is the application of force** |
| 4 | **force changes its motion** |

**Note how sentence 3 is phrased**: the textbook does not define "what force is"; it **names an action students can already perform as force**.
This step connects everyday experience to a physical term, **without introducing any formula**.

### 2 · Give the discipline a name, and correct one point of grammar

> `Explain that the word dynamics comes from a Greek word meaning power.`
> `Also point out that the word dynamics is singular, like the word physics.`

**"Dynamics" is the name of a discipline, and it is singular** — like physics.

### 3 · Introduce three terms that will be used again and again

> `You may want to introduce the terms system, external force, and internal force.`

**These three words appear here for the first time and are needed again for the third law.**
The textbook uses the tone of `You may want to` — **showing that it thinks this step can be taken or left depending on the students**.

## Contraindications

| Who or situation it does not apply to | Basis | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| students who **have not yet studied kinematics** | sentence 4, "force changes its motion", needs an **already established concept of "state of motion"** to be readable | **strong** — follows from the nature of the method |
| students who can already draw force diagrams fluently and only want more problem sets | this method **does not produce problem-solving ability**, it only establishes terminology | **medium** |
| students **outside** this tier (OL) | the textbook tags exactly that passage `[OL]`; the AL and BL tiers get different suggestions | **medium** — the tag clearly draws the scope, but "not tagged" still ≠ "does not apply", it is merely **untested** |

## Risks

| Where it can go wrong | Why |
|---|---|
| telling step 1 as "force makes objects move" | the textbook's sentence 1 already says **objects at rest tend to stay at rest** — what force changes is the **state of motion**, not "making objects move" |
| skipping sentence 3 and giving the definition of `F` directly | that step is **naming the student's action**; skip it and "force" becomes an imported symbol |
| treating that `dynamics` line as small talk | the textbook devotes a whole hint to it, **showing it expects students to take the discipline name as a plural** |
| talking about net force right away | the textbook puts "several forces acting at once" in the `[AL]` tier (see another teaching method) — **the OL tier only goes as far as "one force changes motion"** |
